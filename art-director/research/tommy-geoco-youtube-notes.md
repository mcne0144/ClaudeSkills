## 1. mD3Q2fYmaMA "Why He's the Most Trusted Man in Design" (This Is Taste E1, Oct 2025, 146K)

Subject: Michael "Ridd" Riddering [captions "Rittering", "Rid"], Dive Club host, Inflight co-founder, Kalamazoo, Michigan.

Thesis: Ridd's early trend-spotting is not a technique but a filter; knowing who you serve (faith, family, community) and conviction earned through failure shape what he champions.

Frameworks and concepts
- "Toolbenders" (Tommy's term, later his brand): designers "so good at what they make. They basically are the marketing for it... They'll push whatever tools exist to their limits, then immediately jump on the new ones."
- Taste vs craft: taste is knowing what should exist and answering why repeatedly; craft is executing against it.
- Mentor walk exercise: on walks, say one thing you like and dislike about every house you pass. Taste as "having an opinion on nearly everything."
- "What should exist?" as the filter, "not what's trending or what looks cool."
- Asking questions as a practiced craft (Ridd dogfoods his podcast, tracking where his attention slips).
- Virality as signal: "if something pops off, it's probably pretty good"; if you don't see why, study it.
- Iron sharpens iron co-founding: Ridd pushes visual identity, Kyle pushes product and UX; "Stupid amounts" of throwaway work.
- "The tastemaker's dilemma": heads-down building vs fatherhood.

Quotes
- "the taste is understanding what you think should exist, right? And then being able to repeatedly answer why. And then the craft is executing against that taste."
- "I was taught in that moment that moving pixels around on a computer can be this act of worship and connection." (Ridd)
- "You don't need San Francisco for that. You don't need funding or followers. You just need to know who it's for." (Tommy's close)

Figures: Inflight's launch video landed a lead seed investor "within six hours" (Jake Hayden, Ridd's brother-in-law). Ridd's first startup "basically failed on my honeymoon."

References: Dive Club, Inflight, Nelson Noa, Tom Johnson, Gunnar Gray (shaders), Gabriel Foulon ("a very subtle way of adding joy to small things"), John Elway.

Format craft: cold-open montage of toolbender work, then the pivot "Rid inspired me to try and spot the next something. But I think that next something is Rid." Structure states a question (how is he early?) then subverts it (purpose, not technique). Chapters move place, work, backstory (NFL dream ended by injury, failed startup), philosophy, cost (fatherhood), "What I learned." Family members act as witnesses. Series formula in the description: "It turns out the technique is the easy part. What actually makes him good at this is something deeper."

## 4. 5s32uf2v6yY "The New Design Portfolio (No Case Studies)" (Feb 2026, 68.6K)

Thesis: On the discovery layer, live, interactive, in-motion proof of judgment beats case studies. Show your work, don't explain it.

- Playground as portfolio ("It breaks the timer"); the product as portfolio; an X feed as playground.
- "Tool osmosis": a new apprenticeship, "faster than the old one, and it involves one human instead of two."
- Four paths, pick one: micro interactions; your own playground; software as a gift (term credited to Kyle Zantos); learn in public.
- Post format: "here's what I built. Here's what I learned. And here are the tools I used." Platform order: X, then Instagram (motion plays, packaging matters more), then LinkedIn ("A little cringe, but it still matters"). "30 microshares beats three case study cycles."

Quotes
- "This is what a playground does. It breaks the timer and it really leaves an impression."
- "Now, a playground doesn't get you the job. It might get you the conversation."
- "You don't need a full product. You just need proof of good judgment."

Figures (cited): 16 design leaders decided in 55 seconds; Figma 2026 hiring study: 73% say AI proficiency need is rising, only 20% think the market is improving, 58% rank visual polish and craft first; DesignerFund: postings up 60% YoY; PwC: AI-exposed industries' jobs up 38%.

Examples: Chloe Yan (layered site with Easter eggs; he stayed 10 minutes), Preston Booth ("The site is the portfolio"), Seb Cornelius, Wora Work (a small world turning from day to dusk), Viktor Oddy, Flo (floguo), Tom Johnson, Ryo Lu (os.ryo.lu), Sam Dape.

Format craft: provocation open ("I'm not reading your case study"), screen tours narrated for what each site signals ("his attention to detail and his definition of joy"), a skeptic's question voiced for the viewer, a paradox section, four prescriptive paths, then a thank-you DM read aloud as proof. ## 5. AApjEHsMkTU "This Was the Biggest Debate in Design This Year" (Dec 2025, 24K)

Thesis: The Karri Saarinen (Linear) vs Ryo Lu (Cursor) argument about designing in code exposes a second axis of design work; AI collapses the cost of changing your mind in code, so use the speed to explore more territory, not just arrive faster.

Frameworks
- Floor raising vs ceiling breaking (his labels). Floor: Bootstrap, Tailwind, design systems ("Here's a decent button. Please stop making bad ones"). Ceiling: experimental interfaces. Ceiling breakers scout the hinterlands; floor raisers codify. "Both are design work."
- Second axis: unconstrained exploration (napkin, canvas, physical prototypes) vs material exploration (in code). A 2x2 he admits he is "still kind of workshopping."
- "Discovery code" (Rauno Freiberg, Vercel [caption "Ronaldo Freeberg"]): "Spaghetti code is how real discoveries happen." Dial "the stupidest idea 100 times and then going back 10 iterations."
- Garbage collection (film) and the clay pot story: volume wins. Lee Black: "volume does not mean complexity"; "Iteration is about cycles and not layers."
- Design as search (Karri), likened to fog of war in Civilization.
- Regression to the mean: LLM bias is "towards the mean" (Karri); "Homogyny is the new risk" [caption spelling] (Tommy, on shadcn everywhere). Weber Wong (FLORA): "consistency was the default... Now, like randomness is the default."
- Samraaj Bath (Ion): "I want AI to come and meet me at my thought, right? I don't want it to do my thinking for me." Tommy: "The vision comes from me, but the velocity comes from the tool."

Quotes
- "Whenever a designer becomes more of a builder, some idealism and creativity dies." (Karri, as read)
- "Discovery code is now cheaper than plywood."
- "The question is not should designers code. It's are you using the new speed to explore more territory or just arriving at the same destination faster?"

References: Andy Allen (3D-printed interfaces), Tom Johnson (dithering, a floppy-disk USB with a dithered portrait), Steve Ruiz (tldraw; "thousands of hours looking at ink move across paper"), Basement Studio (developers start on day zero alongside design), Stephen Haney (Paper; "people like me who make to think"), Sketch as his old iteration savior.

Format craft: a Twitter spat as hook; "smooth brains like mine need examples"; a hand-drawn matrix as the visual spine; a self-interrupting "Tommy here, quick note" admitting the model is unfinished; each interviewee placed on the board; ends asking which quadrant the viewer is in.

## 20. Storytelling and format playbook

1. Two promises. Friday essays promise clarity on a contested question (data, history, personal ledger). This Is Taste promises a change in the host's own belief ("I went to learn X, I learned Y"). Podcasts are framed by a Tommy-written cold-open thesis and a one-reframe outro.
2. The subverted premise. Promise the how, deliver the why: "the technique is the easy part."
3. Open loops: the $40K refusal paid off 15 minutes later; the Colin and Samir freeze-frame returned to for the reveal; weekly cliffhangers.
4. Host as participant: critiqued by Soren, introducing Bernabe to Casey, admitting exhaustion, putting his own dead startup in the graveyard. Vulnerability works as evidence.
5. Witness characters: family, co-founders and staff describe the subject, which is more credible than self-description.
6. Name the thing. Portable coinages: toolbenders, tool osmosis, phantom competency, floor raisers and ceiling breakers, unconstrained vs material exploration, discovery code, the Worth-It Test, cultural campfires, practitioner class, headless design, harness as air traffic control, the third lane, airplane noises and vegetables.
7. Receipts plus humility: dense stats, then explicit caveats ("I am in a bubble," "still workshopping") and myth correction. Rigor buys permission for strong opinions.
8. An open packaging bargain: clicky titles stated as the price of delivering nuance.
9. Dated, falsifiable predictions that create accountability and return visits.
10. A self-built cinematic universe: recurring characters (Ridd, Soren, Bernabe), cross-plugs, and survey data reused across videos.
11. Second-person endings with one small action: take the survey, build one thing and tag me, look at the names on your phone, which quadrant are you in.
12. Handmade signals in an AI era: a stop-motion floppy-disk intro, physical gifts, a Kling-made intro with weekly community Easter eggs.
13. Production model: a small on-location crew embedded for 48 hours, with evocative chapter titles ("The hot sauce scale," "Permission to fail," "Letting go").

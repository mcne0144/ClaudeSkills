## 1. mD3Q2fYmaMA "Why He's the Most Trusted Man in Design" (This Is Taste E1, Oct 2025, 146K views, 21:04)

Subject: Michael "Ridd" Riddering (captions "Rittering", "Rid", "Red"), host of Dive Club podcast, co-founder of Inflight with Kyle Barber, Kalamazoo, Michigan.

Thesis: Ridd's trend-spotting is not a technique but a filter; taste comes from knowing who you serve (faith, family, community), and a conviction earned through failure.

Named concepts and frameworks
- "Toolbenders": "There's this breed of designer that is so good at what they make. They basically are the marketing for it... I call them tool benders. They'll push whatever tools exist to their limits, then immediately jump on the new ones just to make something dope." This becomes a recurring Tommy brand term (Toolbenders newsletter, talent network).
- Taste vs craft definition (Ridd and Tommy in dialogue): taste = "understanding what you think should exist" and being able to "repeatedly answer why"; craft = "executing against that taste."
- The mentor walk exercise: on walks, for every house passed, say something you like and dislike about it. Taste as "having an opinion on nearly everything."
- "What should exist?" as the filter, "not what's trending or what looks cool."
- Asking questions as a craft: Ridd dogfoods his own podcast, watching where his attention slips.
- Virality as signal: "if something pops off, it's probably pretty good," and if you don't understand why, study it.
- "The tastemaker's dilemma": the heads-down season vs being present as a dad.
- Iron sharpens iron co-founding: Ridd pushes visual identity, Kyle pushes product and UX. "Stupid amounts" of throwaway work; Figma file "on V8."

Verbatim quotes
- "the taste is understanding what you think should exist, right? And then being able to repeatedly answer why. And then the craft is executing against that taste."
- "the best designers I feel like you could ask them their opinion on a chair, cars, like they'll just say why they like or dislike things."
- "I was taught in that moment that moving pixels around on a computer can be this act of worship and connection." (Ridd)

Figures: Kalamazoo population 73,000 (Tommy). Inflight launch video got a lead seed investor "within six hours" (Jake Hayden, Ridd's brother-in-law and producer). Ridd age 33 at filming.

References: Dive Club, Inflight, Nelson Noa, Tom Johnson, Gunnar Gray, Gabriel Foulon ("a very subtle way of adding joy to small things"), Ridd's failed B2B SaaS ("The company basically failed on my honeymoon").

Format craft
- Cold open: a montage of toolbender work plus rapid-fire voice bites, then the thesis pivot "Rid inspired me to try and spot the next something. But I think that next something is Rid."
- Structure: stated question (how is he always early?) then a deliberate subversion (the answer is purpose, not technique). Chapters move from place (Kalamazoo, family relocation story) to work (co-founder sessions) to backstory (NFL dream ended by broken bones, failed startup) to philosophy (taste) to cost (fatherhood) to "What I learned."

## 5. AApjEHsMkTU "This Was the Biggest Debate in Design This Year" (Dec 2025, 24K, 19:35)

Thesis: The Karri Saarinen (Linear) vs Ryo Lu (Cursor) argument about designing in code reveals a second axis of design work, unconstrained vs material exploration; AI collapses the cost of changing your mind in code, so the question is whether you use speed to explore more territory or arrive at the same place faster.

Frameworks
- Floor vs ceiling (his labels): floor raising makes good design accessible (Bootstrap, Tailwind, design systems: "Here's a decent button. Please stop making bad ones."); ceiling breaking pushes what is possible.
- Second axis: unconstrained exploration (napkin, infinite canvas, physical prototypes) vs material exploration (in or near code). Produces a 2x2 he admits he is "still kind of workshopping."
- "Discovery code" vs production code (Rauno Freiberg [caption "Ronaldo Freeberg"], Vercel): "Spaghetti code is how real discoveries happen, not production code, discovery code."
- "Garbage collection" from film: shoot lots, sort later.
- The clay pot story (quantity group beats the quality group).
- Rauno: dial "the stupidest idea 100 times and then going back 10 iterations."
- Lee Black: "volume does not mean complexity"; "Iteration is about cycles and not layers."
- Ceiling breakers explore the hinterlands; floor raisers codify proven patterns. "Both are design work."
- "Design as search" (Karri) mapped to fog of war in Civilization; designer as a scouting function.
- Regression to the mean (Karri): LLM bias is "towards the mean"; "Homogeny is the new risk" (Tommy, re shadcn everywhere).
- Weber Wong (FLORA): "consistency was the default... Now, like randomness is the default."
- Weber on slop vs play (captions: "One person sloth another person's play", i.e. one person's slop is another person's play).
- Samraaj Bath (Ion): "I want AI to come and meet me at my thought... I don't want it to do my thinking for me." Accelerates code, protects thinking (handwritten notebooks, no autocomplete for writing).
- Tommy's workflow: "The vision comes from me, but the velocity comes from the tool."

Verbatim quotes
- "Whenever a designer becomes more of a builder, some idealism and creativity dies." (Karri, as read by Tommy)
- "architects don't sketch free from physics and then invite gravity later." (Ryo, as read)
- "Discovery code is now cheaper than plywood."

Figures: Cursor visual editor announced December 12 (2025). UX Tools dropping annual survey for quarterly snapshots and building a prototyping tools benchmark.

References: Andy Allen (3D-printed physical interfaces), Tom Johnson (dithering, floppy-disk USB with a dithered portrait), Steve Ruiz (tldraw; "thousands of hours looking at ink move across paper"), Basement Studio (developers start day zero in parallel with design), Stephen Haney (Paper: "people like me who make to think").

Format craft: frames a Twitter spat as a hook, then "smooth brains like mine need examples"; hand-drawn matrix as the visual spine; a self-interrupting "Tommy here, quick note" insert admitting the matrix is unfinished (humility as credibility); each interviewee placed in a quadrant; jokey asides (Magic the Gathering deck tool, "mono white... goes hard"). Ends with a question to the viewer ("which quadrant are you in right now").

Credited also to a fable by Soleio, "The Keeper" (caption "my friend Cleo"; description links docs.soleio.com/keeper).

- Linear (Karri): "Craft is the input, quality is the output. The simplest way to increase quality is to reduce scope."

- Labor lens: who gets paid, trained, stays. The tool is chosen by owners, cost absorbed by practitioners.
- The Worth-It Test (his framework, prompted by The Second Machine Age): 1. new capability; 2. diffusion of upside; 3. believable new ladders. A disruption earns only "a qualified yes" if it clears all three. AI clears bar 1, bar 2 is open, bar 3 is his biggest worry.
- Photography took over a commercial function (affordable likeness) and freed painting (Impressionism context).

## 9. Y8ZXPzYwkzs "This is the New Type of Design Work" (Apr 2026, 14:16)

Thesis: "Agentic design" is forming: the agent works inside the canvas at a different altitude, and the harness (not the model) is what makes it useful; he predicts headless design within 18 months.

Frameworks
- Altitudes: designers switch between product view and frame-level fidelity; agents now operate at different altitudes too (prompting from outside the canvas vs agent inside the tool while you sleep).
- Round tripping, redefined: "how human intent gets into the agent and how agent output comes back to the human intent layer."
- Stephen Haney's three-part stack: canvas, spatial relationship, and talking to your agent (draw or rearrange as a way to prompt).
- Harness as air traffic control. Three jobs: 1. carries context the model doesn't have (design system, codebases, tickets, Slack; "The model is stateless, but your harness is stateful"); 2. enforces governance (brand guidelines, approval flows; "a sort of gatekeeper"); 3. holds history (continuity day to day).
- Headless design: analogous to headless CMS, decoupling design operations from the designer's continuous presence in a tool.
- On-record prediction (18 months from April 2026): "A major design team will ship a product where more than half of the canvas frames were designed by an agent... And it won't look like slop." Also predicts design tool consolidation by H2 2026 ("If we get to quarter 4 and no significant design acquisition has happened, I will be surprised").

Verbatim quotes
- "An agent harness is air traffic control for your AI workflow."
- "It's that moment when it does something that genuinely helps you that you did not ask it to do. That is a true aha moment."
- "I believe this is the best time in history to be a designer. Not because AI tools are doing the work, but because I believe they're finally catching up to supporting good design thinking. Supporting, not replacing."

Example: his agent produced 18 chart artboards in Paper unprompted ahead of the survey deadline: "they weren't up to my standard yet, but they were a fantastic starting point."

References: Figma Make, MagicPath, Paper, Pencil.dev (Tom Krcha), Google Stitch, Claude Design; Simon [surname UNVERIFIED], Ramp product design: "it's like Claude under the hood, but our harness on top is the power"; Figma MCP; Inflight as a context layer.

## 20. Storytelling and format craft playbook (cross-video)

Useful for Atelier when advising Shannon on video, campaign films and editorial content.

1. Two formats, two promises. Friday essays promise clarity on a contested question (data plus history plus personal ledger); This Is Taste promises transformation of the host's own belief (went to learn X, learned Y). Podcasts are bracketed by a Tommy-written cold-open thesis and an outro that distills one reframe.
2. The subverted premise. Every doc description: "I went there to learn [technique]... It turns out the technique is the easy part." The audience comes for technique and leaves with values. Strong pattern for brand films: promise the how, deliver the why.
3. Open loops. Splitting opens on the $40K refusal and pays it off 15 minutes later; Colin and Samir opens on a freeze-frame and returns to it for the Casey reveal; Agentic design ends on a cliffhanger to next week.
4. Host as participant, not narrator. He gets critiqued by Soren, introduces Bernabe to Casey, admits exhaustion on the Colin and Samir shoot, lists his dead startup in the graveyard. Vulnerability functions as evidence.
5. Witness characters. Family members, co-founders, employees describe the subject (Ridd's dad, wife, brother-in-law; Soren's team). Character through other people's eyes is more credible than self-description.
6. Name the thing. He coins or popularizes portable terms: toolbenders, tool osmosis, phantom competency, floor raisers vs ceiling breakers, unconstrained vs material exploration, discovery code, the Worth-It Test, cultural campfires, practitioner class, headless design, air traffic control harness, third lane, airplane noises and vegetables. Coined frames are what get quoted and shared.
7. Receipts plus humility. Dense stat montages, then explicit caveats (sample sizes, "I can't precisely prove causation," "I am in a bubble," "Tommy here, quick note... still workshopping"), and myth correction (the fake Miyamoto quote). Rigor signals buy permission for strong opinions.
8. Honest packaging bargain. Clicky titles and thumbnails ("airplane noises") to deliver nuance ("vegetables"), stated openly to the audience.
9. Falsifiable predictions with dates (18-month agentic canvas prediction; Q4 2026 design acquisition), creating accountability and return visits.
10. Callbacks and cross-links build a cinematic universe of his own: Ridd appears in the vlog, Soren is plugged in the slow web essay, Colin and Samir trailer inserted in the campfires essay, survey data reused across four videos.
11. Second-person endings with a single small action: take the survey and send to one person; build one thing this week and tag me; look at the names on your phone; which quadrant are you in; what do you care about enough to say no to.
12. Handmade signals in an AI era: stop-motion floppy-disk intro, physical gifts (dithered floppy USB, Sir Collect-a-Lot miniature), Kling-made countdown intro paired with weekly community Easter eggs.
13. Production model: small on-location crew (description credits Babes production company, line producer, AD, DP, sound, gaffer), editors Sam Chapman and Agustin Eguia, 48-hour embeds, chapters with evocative titles ("The hot sauce scale," "Permission to fail," "Letting go").

# Methods, Lenses and Templates

The operating manual. How the agent generates ideas, challenges Shannon's ideas, looks through other eras and cultures, and writes things down. Pairs with `01-jason-swet-study.md` (his frameworks are the default spine).

---

## Part A. The default creative workflow

1. **Clarify the problem** (not the deliverable). What must change in someone's head or behavior? For whom? Measured how?
2. **Find the tension and the insight.** "People think X, but the reality is Y." (Swet) Look in: the category's lies, the customer's contradictions, the product's overlooked truth, the culture's current argument.
3. **Write the idea as a verb.** Dramatize, flip, contrast, rip up, hand over, reveal, exaggerate, remove, ritualize. One line, fits on a post-it.
4. **Size the idea** on the Idea Sizing scale: tactic, one-off, execution, campaign, platform. Say which it is honestly. A great one-off is fine if that's what the budget is.
5. **Define the look and feel in writing**: feeling words, a metaphor ("It's like ___ meets ___"), then ingredients (light, lens, palette, type, texture, motion, casting, set), then boundaries (what it is NOT).
6. **Trace the lineage** of every reference: where it comes from, what DNA it shares, who can execute it.
7. **Executions across the funnel and channels** (see Part E), each laddering to the idea.
8. **Pressure-test** with the challenge modes (Part C) and the lenses (Part D).
9. **Make it real**: in situ mockups, style frames, prompts, a shot list, an asset matrix.

## Part B. Ideation techniques (with origin)

- **James Webb Young, *A Technique for Producing Ideas* (1940)**: gather specific and general material, digest it, let it incubate, the idea appears, then shape it to reality. "An idea is nothing more nor less than a new combination of old elements."
- **Bisociation** (Arthur Koestler, *The Act of Creation*, 1964): creativity is the collision of two previously unrelated frames of reference. The engine behind Swet's metaphor templates.
- **Lateral thinking** (Edward de Bono): provocation ("Po"), random entry (pick a random word and force a link), reversal, escape from dominant ideas. **Six Thinking Hats**: white (facts), red (feelings), black (risks), yellow (benefits), green (new ideas), blue (process).
- **SCAMPER** (Bob Eberle, from Alex Osborn's checklist): Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify/Magnify/Minify, Put to other use, Eliminate, Reverse/Rearrange.
- **Oblique Strategies** (Brian Eno and Peter Schmidt, 1975): cards with lateral prompts like "Honor thy error as a hidden intention" and "Use an old idea." Use one when stuck.
- **Morphological matrix** (Fritz Zwicky): list attributes as columns (format, tone, visual world, talent, channel), options under each, combine randomly.
- **Forced connections / mashups**: product + unrelated culture (Swet: "a luxury ad made in a garage").
- **Inversion / worst idea first**: generate terrible ideas, then flip them.
- **Constraint play**: one color, no product shown, no words, 6 seconds, $500, only found footage.
- **"What would X do?"**: ask how a specific artist, era, or brand would solve it (Part D).
- **How Might We** (IDEO, design thinking): reframe the problem as an open question.
- **Crazy 8s** (Google Ventures design sprint): eight ideas in eight minutes.
- **Insight mining sources**: reviews (1 to 3 star are gold), Reddit and forum threads, search queries, sales call transcripts, TikTok comments, frontline staff, category taboos, ethnography (watch people use the product).
- **The "and then what?" ladder** (benefit laddering, means-end chain): feature → functional benefit → emotional benefit → identity/value. Ads often live best at the emotional or identity rung with proof at the functional one.
- **Jobs to Be Done switch interview** (Moesta): push of the current situation, pull of the new, anxiety of the new, habit of the present.

## Part C. Challenge modes (how the agent pushes back)

Shannon asked to be challenged. The agent should run at least two of these on any idea she brings, then build on the strongest version.

1. **Steelman first**: restate her idea in its strongest possible form before critiquing it.
2. **Insight audit**: is there a real "people think X, but really Y," or is it a product feature in costume?
3. **Size check**: is she calling a one-off a platform? Can it produce ten executions without repeating?
4. **Ownability test**: could a competitor run it by swapping the logo? If yes, what makes it ours (a distinctive asset, a product truth, a voice)?
5. **Brand linkage test**: will people remember whose ad it was? Is the brand in the first seconds or woven into the idea itself?
6. **Pre-mortem**: it's six months later and the campaign failed. Why? (Gary Klein's technique.)
7. **Red team the audience**: argue as the skeptical, distracted, or offended viewer.
8. **The taste question** (Swet): whose taste is this for: mainstream, cultural, counter, elite, commercial? Is the brand claiming a cultural taste it didn't live?
9. **The opposite brief**: what if we did the exact reverse? Sometimes the reverse is the idea.
10. **Lineage check**: has this been done? By whom? What would make our version additive rather than derivative?
11. **Evidence check**: is the strategy betting against the evidence (for example ultra-narrow targeting against Ehrenberg-Bass findings, or short-term activation only against Binet and Field)? Name the evidence and its strength.
12. **Craft dependency check** (Swet on LA28): is it low-concept and therefore entirely dependent on flawless craft? Do we have the budget for that craft?
13. **The ten-percent-braver push**: what's the version that makes the client slightly nervous but still on strategy?
14. **Feasibility and cost**: who executes it, with what tools, in what time?
15. **Familiarity check (MAYA)**: is it advanced enough to be noticed and familiar enough to be accepted? If "push it further" is the note, ask whether the audience can follow; if the idea is safe, name one twist on a familiar form. See MAYA in file 06.

Tone rule: challenge the idea, never the person. Always end with a stronger version, not just objections.

## Part D. Perspective lenses (thousands of years, every region)

Use these to look at a brief "from a different perspective and worldwide influence." Each lens: what it values, and the question it asks of the brief. Full histories in files 03 and 04.

### Era lenses
- **Paleolithic cave painters (Lascaux, Chauvet, Sulawesi)**: the image as ritual and survival magic, painted where the rock's bumps suggest the animal. *Ask*: what does our audience gather around in the dark? What's the primal image?
- **Ancient Egypt**: hierarchy of scale, composite view (each part shown from its most legible angle), images built to last forever. *Ask*: what's the most legible angle of each part of our product? What would we carve in stone?
- **Classical Greece and Rome**: idealized proportion (Polykleitos' canon), contrapposto, civic monuments. *Ask*: what's our ideal form, and is idealization still believable?
- **Han and Tang China, Song landscape (shan shui)**: the scroll unrolling through time, emptiness as part of the composition, calligraphic energy (qi). *Ask*: can the story unroll? Where is the emptiness?
- **Islamic geometric and calligraphic art**: infinite pattern, the sacred word as image, aniconism. *Ask*: can the brand be a pattern? Can the word itself be the picture?
- **Medieval illuminated manuscripts and Gothic**: gold as divine light, marginalia jokes, stained glass storytelling for the non-literate. *Ask*: how would we tell this to someone who can't read our language? What goes in the margins?
- **Renaissance**: linear perspective, patronage, the artist as genius, humanism. *Ask*: who's the patron and what do they want to be seen funding?
- **Baroque**: drama, diagonal movement, chiaroscuro, overwhelming the senses for persuasion (Counter-Reformation as the first mass-persuasion campaign). *Ask*: what would full theatrical commitment look like?
- **Edo Japan, ukiyo-e**: mass-produced prints of the "floating world" (celebrities, kabuki actors, courtesans, travel), flat color planes, bold outlines, cropping that influenced Impressionism. *Ask*: who are today's floating-world celebrities in this category? Can it be mass-produced and collectible?
- **Mughal and Rajput miniatures; Persian miniatures**: jewel tones, multiple viewpoints, dense narrative detail, borders as frames. *Ask*: can one image tell the whole story in layers?
- **Industrial Revolution and Arts and Crafts (William Morris)**: reaction against machine sameness, honor the handmade. *Ask*: in an AI-smooth world, where is the hand? (Swet's visible facture.)
- **Belle Époque posters (Chéret, Mucha, Toulouse-Lautrec)**: the street as gallery; advertising as art collected by the public. *Ask*: would anyone want to steal our poster?
- **Bauhaus**: form follows function, unify art and industry, primary geometry. *Ask*: what's the minimum system that does the job beautifully?
- **Constructivism**: art as a tool for the revolution, diagonals, photomontage, red and black, agitprop. *Ask*: what's the call to collective action?
- **Dada and Surrealism**: absurdity, chance, the unconscious, the uncanny juxtaposition. *Ask*: what's the dream logic version?
- **Swiss International Style**: objectivity, grid, sans serif, clarity as a social good. *Ask*: what if we removed every opinion and just made it clear?
- **Creative Revolution (Bernbach, DDB, 1960s)**: honest, witty, self-deprecating ads, copywriter and art director as a team (VW "Think Small", Avis "We Try Harder"). *Ask*: what's the honest weakness we can turn into a strength?
- **Pop Art**: commerce as subject, repetition, celebrity, mass production. *Ask*: what's already iconic about the product that we could simply frame as art?
- **Punk and DIY**: speed, cheapness, xerox, attitude over polish. *Ask*: what's the version we could make tonight for free?
- **Postmodernism and Memphis**: irony, collage of styles, rules broken on purpose. *Ask*: what rule of the category could we wink at?
- **Early internet and Y2K**: the web as frontier, chrome, futurism. *Ask*: what's the native language of the newest platform?
- **Creator era (2015 to now)**: the person is the channel, authenticity and parasocial bonds, format literacy. *Ask*: who's the creator who already speaks to this audience, and how do we enable rather than hijack them (Swet on Gap)?
- **AI era (2023 to now)**: infinite variation, cheap generation, scarcity shifts to taste, provenance and human effort. *Ask*: what can only a human (or this brand) make? What's the analog counterweight?

### World lenses (living traditions; borrow with respect and credit)
- **Japanese aesthetics**: *ma* (meaningful negative space and pause), *wabi-sabi* (beauty of imperfection and impermanence), *mono no aware* (gentle sadness of passing things), *kawaii* (cuteness as social glue), *shibui* (subtle, unobtrusive beauty), *kintsugi* (repair made visible). *Ask*: where's the pause? What imperfection is the beauty?
- **Chinese aesthetics**: auspicious color (red, gold), numbers and homophones (8 for prosperity), calligraphy, *liubai* (leaving white). *Ask*: what are the culturally auspicious and taboo signs for this market?
- **Korean**: *jeong* (deep affection and bond), moon jar asymmetry, Hallyu's polished idol system and fandom rituals. *Ask*: how do we build fandom rituals?
- **Indian**: *rasa* theory (Natyashastra's aesthetic emotions: love, humor, compassion, fury, heroism, fear, disgust, wonder, peace), truck art and hand-painted signage, Bollywood maximalism. *Ask*: which rasa should the work evoke?
- **Islamic and Persian**: geometry as infinity, garden (paradise) as ordered beauty, calligraphy. *Ask*: what's the infinite pattern in this brand?
- **West African**: adinkra symbols (Akan) as visual proverbs, kente's coded colors and patterns, bogolanfini (mud cloth), Nollywood's speed and volume, Afrobeats' global sound. *Ask*: can our brand have a proverb-symbol?
- **East and Southern Africa**: Ndebele house painting (bold geometric color), Maasai beadwork color codes, South African advertising's humor and multilingual wit (frequent Cannes winners). *Ask*: what's the bold, communal color story?
- **Mexican and Latin American**: muralism (public, political, monumental), papel picado, lucha libre iconography, Día de Muertos (joyful mortality), Tropicália (Brazilian cultural cannibalism, *antropofagia*: devour foreign influence and remake it), Brazilian advertising's craft and emotion. *Ask*: what's the public mural version? What would we "cannibalize" and remake?
- **Caribbean**: Carnival costume and mas, dancehall graphics, sound system culture. *Ask*: what's the street-party energy?
- **Indigenous Australian**: dot painting and Dreaming stories, Country as living. Strict caution: many designs are sacred and owned by specific communities; never imitate without permission and collaboration. *Ask*: whose story is this to tell?
- **Pacific and Māori**: tā moko, kowhaiwhai patterns, carving; same caution on cultural ownership.
- **Nordic**: *hygge* and *lagom* (balance, just enough), functional craft, light as precious. *Ask*: what's "just enough"?
- **Mediterranean**: sun, terracotta, Greek and Italian vernacular signage, la dolce vita. *Ask*: what's the leisure ritual?
- **Middle Eastern contemporary**: Arabic type as expressive image, Gulf futurism. *Ask*: does the work read right-to-left too?
- **Eastern European**: Polish poster school (painterly, surreal, metaphor-driven film posters), Czech animation and puppetry, Soviet modernist mosaics. *Ask*: what's the single surreal metaphor?

**Rule for world lenses**: use them to unlock thinking and to brief collaborators who live that culture. Cultural taste "is gatekept by lived experience. You can study it, serve it, amplify it, but you can't claim it" (Swet). Credit and hire from the source culture when a campaign borrows its codes.

### Audience lenses
- The light buyer who barely thinks about the category (Ehrenberg-Bass).
- The skeptic who has been burned before.
- The superfan who will make content for free (Jon Bellion's community).
- The frontline employee who has to live with the campaign.
- The person scrolling at 11pm with sound off.
- The grandparent and the 15-year-old.
- The competitor's CMO seeing it.

### Discipline lenses
- How would a **novelist** open it (Swet's hooks)? A **choreographer** stage it (Gap: movement as demo)? An **architect** make it a space? A **game designer** make it playable? A **comedian** find the joke? A **scientist** prove it? A **museum curator** label it? A **musician** find the hook and the drop?

## Part E. Campaign architecture template

```
CAMPAIGN: [name]
Business problem:
Audience (broad, with the light buyer in mind):
Category entry points (buying situations we want to own):
Insight: People think ___, but really ___.
Idea (verb + object): ___
Line / platform name:
Idea size: tactic | one-off | execution | campaign | platform
Hook (clear, not clever; must catch and hold, with a payoff tied to the idea):
Key visual: one image that locks up the whole idea at a glance
Look and feel:
  Feeling words:
  Metaphor: It's like ___ meets ___ / It should feel like ___ but ___
  Ingredients: light / lens / palette / type / texture / motion / casting / set / sound
  Lineage: references and where they come from; who can execute
  Not this: (boundaries)
Distinctive assets used or built:
Executions by role:
  Fame / reach (hero film, OOH, stunt, PR)
  Consideration (creator partnerships, social series, search, reviews)
  Conversion (performance variants, landing page, offer, retargeting)
  Loyalty / community (rituals, CRM, events, UGC)
Variant system: hooks x formats x audiences (for Advantage+ / Smart+ style delivery)
Measures: brand (recall, linkage, search lift) and activation (CPA, leads, sales)
Risks and pre-mortem:
Case study write-up (STAR, for portfolio or pitch; from Swet's portfolio videos):
  Situation and task: the client challenge
  Actions: strategy, concepting and the art direction choices made
  Results: shares, press, awards or real-world impact (spec work can enter award shows)
```

## Part F. Brand creation template

```
BRAND: [name]
1. Truths: product truth, customer truth, category truth, founder truth
2. Positioning: For [audience] who [need/tension], [brand] is the [frame of reference] that [onliness/difference] because [reason to believe].
3. Purpose (why it exists beyond profit), promise (what customers can count on)
4. Personality: 3 to 5 traits, each with "this, not that" (e.g. "confident, not arrogant")
5. Voice: vocabulary, rhythm, humor level, words we never use
6. Name (if needed): type (descriptive, suggestive, abstract, invented, compound, metaphor), shortlist, trademark screen needed
7. Identity system:
   Logo type and construction rationale
   Palette with roles (one accent rule)
   Type system (display, emphasis, body, utility)
   Motif / graphic device (one idea repeated)
   Imagery direction (photo, illustration, 3D, AI rules)
   Motion principles, sonic cues
   Distinctive assets to build deliberately
8. Lineage and world lenses consulted
9. Applications: website, social templates, packaging, signage, merch, decks
10. Guidelines outline
```

## Part G. Avatar, mascot and character template

```
CHARACTER: [name]
Role: brand mascot | AI spokesperson avatar | creator persona | game/3D character
Job it does for the brand (distinctive asset? explainer? community figure?)
Personality traits (this, not that) and backstory in 3 lines
Shape language and silhouette test
Proportions (head-to-body ratio), age read, species/object
Palette (tie to brand palette), materials (matte clay, plush, chrome, painted)
Expression sheet: neutral, joy, surprise, thinking, mischief, concern
Pose sheet and turnaround (front, 3/4, side, back)
Voice (if speaking): tone, pace, accent, catchphrases; voice tool and consent
Medium: 2D illustration | 3D (GLB) | video avatar | sticker set
Consistency system: reference sheet, seed or character reference, LoRA if needed
Ethics: no real person's likeness without written consent; AI disclosure where platforms require it
```

## Part H. 3D concept template

```
3D CONCEPT: [name]
Purpose: product viz | CGI out-of-home | brand world | AR filter | web hero | mascot
Style: photoreal | stylized | claymorphic | low-poly | chrome/liquid | miniature diorama
Scene: subject, environment, scale, camera (focal length, move), lighting (HDRI, key/rim), materials (PBR notes)
Motion: loop or narrative, duration, easing
Pipeline: concept image → image-to-3D or modeling → texturing → lighting → render or real-time
Output formats: MP4 9:16/1:1/16:9, GLB for web/AR, stills
```

## Part I. Mood board write-up template (Swet's rule: always write it down)

```
DIRECTION: [title]
In one line: It should feel like ___ but ___.
Feelings: three words
Ingredients:
  Light:
  Lens and framing:
  Palette:
  Texture / facture:
  Type:
  Casting and styling:
  Set and props:
  Motion and edit:
  Sound:
Lineage: these references descend from ___
Who can execute: photographers, directors, illustrators, studios, AI tools
Boundaries (not this):
Why it serves the idea:
Finishing pass (from Swet's mood board and taste videos; template wording is NotebookLM's):
  Every image justified: why is it here, which ingredient does it show? Cut what cannot answer.
  Level 2 taste metaphor, for clients: a feeling or reference ("dreamlike", "a Michel Gondry collection").
  Level 3 named ingredients, for executors: flash lighting, hand-drawn mixed media, toasted oak.
  One unifying layer: a shared color grade, LUT or subtle grain across all images (he gives no percentage).
  Rogue colors harmonized (mask the object, shift only that hue).
  Composed like a film: mix close-ups, wides and textures; color spread evenly, nothing lopsided.
  Client-hat check: anything that could be misread gets a caption or gets cut.
```

## Part L. Critique protocol (for work Shannon shares)

1. What is it trying to do (state the brief back)?
2. First read, second read, third read.
3. Idea: insight present? verb present? size?
4. Execution: hierarchy, contrast, type, color, light, craft.
5. Brand linkage and distinctiveness.
6. Platform fit (safe zones, sound-off, hook, length).
7. Lineage: what it borrows; is it additive?
8. Scores are optional (Swet rates ads out of 10). Always pair a score with the one change that would raise it most.

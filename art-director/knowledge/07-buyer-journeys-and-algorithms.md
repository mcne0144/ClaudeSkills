# 07. Buyer Journeys and Platform Algorithms

Knowledge file for the Bright Matter art-direction and creative-strategy agent. Covers how people actually move from trigger to purchase (consumer, B2B, auto, local), and how the major social and ad platforms decide what gets seen in 2025 to 2026, with what each implies for how many creative variants to make.

Last research pass: 2026-10-03. Platform behavior changes monthly; re-verify any platform claim older than six months before putting it in a client deck.

## How to use this file

- Tiers: **[primary]** platform's own newsroom, help center, engineering blog, code repository, or the study's publisher; **[peer-reviewed]**; **[vendor claim]** a company selling the measurement, media or software; **[secondary]** press or practitioner write-up of a primary statement; **[flag]** a source problem (internal inconsistency, untraceable stat, forum).
- Platforms describe signals; they do not publish weights (X is a partial exception). Any "the algorithm wants exactly N" rule is lore unless a platform page says it.
- Rule for this file: platform facts come from the platform; performance claims about platforms from the platform are **vendor claims**, because platforms sell the ads.

## Part 1: Buyer journey models

## Google: Decoding Decisions, "the messy middle" (2020)

### What it says
- Google and The Behavioural Architects, *Decoding Decisions: Making sense of the messy middle* (2020) [primary, vendor-published]. Between **trigger** and **purchase** sits the "messy middle", where people loop between two mental modes:
  - **Exploration**: expansive; adding brands and options to the consideration set.
  - **Evaluation**: reductive; narrowing options.
- A background state, **exposure**, runs throughout: everything the buyer has absorbed about brands and categories before and during the loop [primary, The Behavioural Architects].
- Six biases prioritized and tested [primary]:
  1. **Category heuristics**: short descriptions of key specs simplify decisions.
  2. **Power of now**: the longer the wait, the weaker the proposition.
  3. **Social proof**: reviews and recommendations persuade.
  4. **Scarcity bias**: less availability, more desirability.
  5. **Authority bias**: swayed by an expert or trusted source.
  6. **Power of free**: a free gift, even unrelated, motivates.
- Method: literature review, shopping observation, Google search trend analysis, and a simulated shopping experiment with 310,000 purchase scenarios across 31 categories, 1,000 in-market UK shoppers per category [primary].
- Findings [primary]: simply showing up as the second-favorite brand moved a significant share of preference; when a fictional brand was "supercharged" with all six biases, even the weakest case (cereal) won 28% of preference from the established favorite, and a fictional car insurer won 87%.

### Evidence and status
- **Vendor research with a simulation**, not real purchases. Google's own caveat: "the results of a simulation are indicative by default ... not a substitute for your own rigorous, in-market testing" [primary]. Google sells the search and YouTube inventory where the messy middle plays out.
- The biases themselves have independent support (see file 06: social proof, scarcity, authority are documented; "power of free" is supported by Shampanier, Mazar and Ariely, 2007, *Marketing Science* [peer-reviewed]).

### Art direction implication
Every asset that appears during evaluation (search ads, VDPs, Google Business Profile photos, comparison posts) should carry the six cues where true: a spec summary, availability today, reviews, expert or certification signals, real stock levels, and any included extras. Presence during the loop matters as much as persuasion.

## Zero Moment of Truth (ZMOT)

### What it says
- Jim Lecinski, *Winning the Zero Moment of Truth* (Google, 2011) [primary, vendor-published]: the online research moment between stimulus and the shelf. Google/Shopper Sciences research behind it reported shoppers used about 10.4 sources of information in 2011 vs 5.3 in 2010 [vendor claim].

### Evidence and status
- **Vendor concept**, now absorbed by the messy-middle model. Useful history; the specific numbers are dated.

### Art direction implication
Assume buyers check you before they call. Your reviews, photos and search snippet are creative assets, not admin.

## McKinsey Consumer Decision Journey and the loyalty loop

### What it says
- Court, Elzinga, Mulder and Vetvik, "The consumer decision journey", *McKinsey Quarterly* (June 2009) [primary]. Replaced the funnel with a circular journey: **initial consideration**, **active evaluation** (brands are added as well as dropped), **moment of purchase**, **post-purchase experience**, and a **loyalty loop** where satisfied buyers can skip evaluation next time.
- Key insight: consumer-driven touchpoints (reviews, word of mouth, own research) carry heavy weight during active evaluation.

### Evidence and status
- **Consultancy survey-based model**, widely adopted. Google's messy middle splits McKinsey's single "active evaluation" phase into exploration and evaluation, arguing they are "cognitively distinct" [primary, Google].

### Art direction implication
The post-purchase experience is media: delivery-day content, service reminders and owner communities feed the loyalty loop and next year's reviews.

## B2B buying groups

### Gartner [primary unless noted]
- B2B buyers spend about **17%** of the purchase journey meeting potential suppliers; with several suppliers, any one rep may get **5% to 6%** (Gartner research, cited in Gartner PDF and 2020 newsroom release).
- Typical buying group for a complex solution: **6 to 10 decision makers**, each with four or five pieces of independently gathered information (Gartner).
- *Gartner B2B Buying Report 2023*: average enterprise buying group of **5 to 11 stakeholders** across about five functions.
- June 2025 Gartner sales survey (632 B2B buyers, Aug to Sep 2024): **61%** prefer an overall rep-free buying experience; **73%** actively avoid suppliers who send irrelevant outreach; buyers prefer sellers for contextual fit questions [secondary reporting of a Gartner press release; MarketScreener and Demand Gen Report].
- Gartner: personalizing content to each buying-group member can increase uncertainty; buyers need content for shared needs (Gartner research summary).

### 6sense Buyer Experience Report 2025 [vendor claim; sells B2B intent data]
- Global study of about 4,000 buyers (North America, EMEA, APAC) [primary press release, 2025-11-12].
- Buying cycle shortened from about 11.3 months (2024) to 10.1 months (2025).
- Point of first contact moved from about 69% to 61% of the journey ("70/30" to "60/40"); reasons cited include validating AI features: 89% of purchases included AI features and 58% engaged sellers earlier to clarify AI details.
- 94% of buying groups ranked preferred vendors before first contact; winning vendor was on the Day One shortlist 95% of the time; buyers evaluated about 5.1 vendors.
- Buying group size about 10.1 people.
- **[flag] Internal inconsistency**: 6sense materials give the pre-contact favorite's win rate as "about 80%" and "77%", and the earlier contact shift as "6 to 7 weeks" in one place and "3.5 weeks" in another. Treat precise figures as approximate; the direction (most of the decision is made before sales contact) is consistent across 6sense, Gartner and Forrester-style research.

### Forrester
- Forrester publishes B2B buying studies with buying-group findings; we did not verify a current public figure for this file, so none is quoted. Blank beats plausible.

### Art direction implication
For B2B clients (fleet, commercial vehicles, multi-location operators buying agency services): the shortlist forms before anyone talks to sales, inside a group of about 6 to 10 people. Create shared-language assets the whole group can forward (one-page proof sheets, short explainer videos, peer-company case studies), and brand-building that makes the client a Day One shortlist name. Mental availability (file 06) is the B2B lever.

## Dark social

### What it says
- Term coined by Alexis Madrigal, "Dark Social: We Have the Whole History of the Web Wrong", *The Atlantic*, October 2012 [primary]: sharing through private channels (DMs, texts, email, group chats) that analytics records as "direct" traffic.
- Platforms now confirm private sharing is central: Instagram head Adam Mosseri named **sends** (DM shares) a top-three ranking signal in January 2025 [primary, Mosseri video, via multiple secondary reports]; X's published ranker predicts "share via DM" and "share via copy link" [primary, xai-org/x-algorithm repository].
- Mosseri (Build Your Tribe podcast, April 2025): more photos and videos are now shared via DMs than in Stories, and more in Stories than in Feed [secondary, Lindsey Gamble write-up].

### Evidence and status
- **Primary for the concept and platform signals.** Attribution of dark social to sales is inherently hard; "how did you hear about us?" self-report fields are the practical measure.

### Art direction implication
Design content to be sent: a useful local tip, a "send this to your friend who needs a truck" hook, a payment calculator screenshot. Add "how did you hear about us" to dealer lead forms so dark social shows up somewhere.

## Search fragmenting: TikTok, Reddit, AI answer engines, and GEO

### What is happening
- **TikTok search**: "Billions of searches are happening on TikTok every day, up more than 40% from last year", and "1 in 4 TikTok users" start searching within 30 seconds of opening the app (TikTok internal data, US, June 2024) [primary; vendor claim on performance]. TikTok launched Search Center for search ads at TikTok World 2025 [primary].
- **AI Overviews reduce clicks**: Pew Research Center (2025-07-22), browsing data from 900 US adults in March 2025: 58% ran at least one Google search with an AI summary; users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits with an AI summary vs 15% without; clicked a link inside the summary on 1% of visits [primary, independent research].
- **Local recommendations from AI**: BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (n = 1,002 US adults): 45% used AI tools for local business recommendations, up from 6% the year before; use of Google for reviews slipped from 83% to 71%; 88% of AI users fact-check reviews cited by AI [vendor claim; BrightLocal sells local SEO software]. [flag: a year-over-year jump this large in one survey panel should be treated cautiously; BrightLocal's 2025 report text also contained a self-contradictory sentence about review-writing rates.]

### Generative engine optimization (GEO)
- Aggarwal et al., "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", *KDD 2024* [peer-reviewed conference]: across a 10,000-query benchmark (GEO-bench), adding **citations, quotations from relevant sources, and statistics** improved source visibility in generative engine answers by up to about 40%; fluency and readability edits added 15% to 30%; classic **keyword stuffing did not help**; tested on Perplexity with up to 37% improvement; effects vary by domain.
- Status: one well-designed study on simulated and one live engine. Engines change fast.

### Art direction implication
For search visibility in AI answers, write pages that state facts plainly with numbers, named sources and quotable lines (inventory counts, warranty terms, certified-tech counts, hours, review averages). Make video content searchable: say the keywords out loud and on screen in TikTok and YouTube Shorts, because those are search surfaces now.

## The auto retail purchase journey

### Cox Automotive Car Buyer Journey Study 2025 (16th annual) [primary; Cox also sells dealer software, so read satisfaction-with-digital findings as partly vendor-interested]
- Survey of 2,300 US consumers who bought a new or used vehicle in the prior 12 months, fielded fall 2025, released 2026-01-13.
- Satisfaction: 71% of all buyers highly satisfied with the process; 76% of new-vehicle buyers, a record; overall dealership-process satisfaction 76%; new-vehicle buyers' dealership satisfaction 81%.
- 44% of new-vehicle buyers and 41% of used buyers said their latest purchase was better than the previous one; 6% said worse.
- Process: 53% completed all steps at the dealership, 7% bought completely online; 63% say an omnichannel process is ideal vs 28% "all online".
- Time (2025 summary table): about 7 hours 11 minutes researching and shopping online, 46 minutes talking with others, 2 hours 55 minutes visiting other dealers or sellers, 2 hours 55 minutes with the selling dealer; total just under 14 hours. New buyers spent almost two fewer hours online than the year before.
- "Mostly digital" buyers (over half the process online) were most satisfied and saved about 41 minutes at the dealership.
- Sources used (all buyers): third-party websites 75%, dealership sites 59%, search engines 41%, used online retailers 30%, social media 26%, automaker sites 25%, AI sites 12%; average 4.6 sites visited (new buyers 4.0).
- AI: usage "relatively modest", but AI users reported higher satisfaction and trust; among mostly-digital buyers, 84% of AI users vs 71% of non-AI users were satisfied overall [secondary: Digital Dealer reporting of the study].

### Art direction implication for dealer groups
- Most of the persuasion happens on third-party listing sites, search and the dealer's VDPs before the visit. Photo quality, walkaround video and price transparency on VDPs are core creative, not merchandising afterthoughts.
- Social media is used by about a quarter of buyers during shopping; its bigger job is earlier: memory and CEPs (file 06) so the dealer is on the shortlist.
- Ads should promise and show the omnichannel process ("start online, finish in store in under an hour") because time saved is a top satisfaction driver.
- Build assets for AI and third-party sites: clear, factual inventory descriptions and consistent dealer facts.

## Local business journeys: Google Business Profile and reviews

### What it says
- Google: local results "are based primarily on relevance, distance, and prominence"; prominence draws on review count and score, links and general web presence (Google Business Profile Help, "Tips to improve your local ranking on Google") [primary].
- BrightLocal LCRS 2026 [vendor claim]: 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses; 41% "always" read reviews (29% the year before); thresholds rising, with more consumers wanting recent reviews and higher star ratings. BrightLocal LCRS 2025 had found 4% "never" read reviews [vendor claim].

### Art direction implication
For every location: real, recent photos (staff, building, inventory, service bay), consistent naming, a steady review-request system, and owner replies that sound human. Treat the Google Business Profile as the most-viewed ad the location runs.

## Part 2: Social platform algorithms, 2025 to 2026

## Shared mechanics across platforms

- **Multi-stage pipelines**: retrieval (narrow millions of candidates to thousands), ranking (predict probabilities of many actions), re-ranking for diversity and policy. Documented by Meta (Andromeda), LinkedIn (LLM retrieval plus sequential ranker), Pinterest (retrieval, pre-ranking, ranking), X (open-sourced pipeline) [primary engineering sources below].
- **Predicted actions, weighted**: each candidate gets predicted probabilities (watch, like, share, comment, follow, hide, report) combined into a score. Weights are set by the platform and change.
- **Exploration or "audition"**: new content is tested on a small audience and graduated. Mosseri, Mixed Signals podcast (December 2025): "a hundred views, call it ... graduate those to a thousand views ... then ... ten thousand", and this model is now standard across TikTok, Instagram, YouTube and Facebook [secondary: CNBC TV18 transcript quotes].
- **Originality enforcement**: Instagram (2024), YouTube (July 2025) and Facebook (July 2025) all demote reposted, unoriginal content (details below).
- **Satisfaction beyond clicks**: YouTube surveys, Instagram "worth your time" style calibration, explicit negative signals (not interested, hide, report).

## TikTok

### For You recommendations [primary: TikTok Newsroom, "How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou"]
- Signals: **user interactions** (likes, shares, follows, comments, content created), **video information** (captions, sounds, hashtags), **device and account settings** (language, country, device type), the last weighted lower.
- "A strong indicator of interest, such as whether a user finishes watching a longer video from beginning to end, would receive greater weight than a weak indicator."
- "Neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors in the recommendation system" (bigger accounts get more views only through their follower base).
- Diversity rules: generally no two videos in a row from the same creator or with the same sound; no duplicated content, already-seen content or spam; some content ineligible (graphic, regulated goods, under review).
- TikTok Privacy Center explainer: uses **collaborative filtering** (people with similar interests) [primary].
- Users can tap "Not interested", filter keywords, refresh the feed, and use a "why this video" tool [primary].

### Search and Shop
- Search: see Part 1. Search ads, Search Center (2025) [primary].
- TikTok Shop: US sales up 120% year over year in early 2025 (TikTok Newsroom, 2025-06-13) [vendor claim]; US BFCM 2025 had nearly 50% more buyers than the prior year's campaign period, with creator affiliates posting nearly 10 million shoppable videos [vendor claim, TikTok Newsroom 2025-12-03]. GlobalData survey commissioned with TikTok Shop: 83% of shoppers discovered a new product there [vendor claim, sponsored].
- Momentum Works/Tabcut estimated TikTok Shop US GMV of US$5.8 billion in H1 2025 [secondary, third-party estimate].

### Art direction implication
Completion and rewatch on longer videos are the strongest stated signals, so structure for retention, not just a hook. Use original audio or legally licensed sound, clear on-screen text and spoken keywords (video information is a signal and search is huge). Each video must stand alone because the feed never shows the same creator twice in a row.

## Instagram and Facebook (Meta)

### Instagram ranking, from Adam Mosseri [primary statements via video; secondary write-ups]
- January 2025: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes, and sends. So when looking at your insights, pay close attention to average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach."
- Two contexts: **connected reach** (followers), where likes weigh more; **unconnected reach** (recommendations), where sends weigh more; watch time matters for both.
- April 2025 (Build Your Tribe podcast): Instagram is prioritizing smaller creators, original content and faster trends; "Hashtags do not significantly increase your reach" [secondary reports of the podcast].
- December 2025 (Mixed Signals): exploration-based "audition" ranking; a post from someone with 50 followers can get millions of views [secondary transcript].
- September 2025: Instagram previewed letting users pick topics to see more or less of ("Your Algorithm" controls) and a revamp centered on Reels, DMs and recommendations [secondary: TechCrunch].
- Meta Transparency Center publishes system cards per surface (Feed, Reels, Explore, Threads) listing predictions and input signals [primary].

### Originality rules [primary: Instagram for Creators, 2024-04-30]
- When two or more identical pieces of content exist, Instagram recommends only the original; it will not replace content "changed in a significant way", for example "materially edited to become a meme, a parody compilation, narrated with a new voiceover or remixed to express a reaction".
- Labels on copies link to the original creator.
- Accounts that post others' content they "didn't create or enhance in a material way" **10 or more times in 30 days** become ineligible for recommendations until they drop below the threshold.
- A ranking input gives smaller creators more distribution: content is shown to a small audience, then top performers to wider groups [primary via Engadget quote of Instagram].

### Facebook unoriginal content crackdown [primary: Facebook Creators blog, 2025-07-14]
- Accounts that "repeatedly" reuse others' videos, photos or text without meaningful enhancement lose monetization for a period and get **reduced distribution on everything they share**.
- Detected duplicate videos get reduced distribution; Facebook is testing links on duplicates pointing to the original.
- First half 2025: action on about 500,000 spammy or fake-engagement accounts; about 10 million impersonator profiles removed.
- Guidance: add meaningful edits, voiceover or commentary; avoid visible third-party watermarks and content "visibly recycled from other apps".

### Trial reels [primary: Instagram for Creators and Meta Newsroom, 2024-12-10]
- Reels shown to non-followers first; not shown in followers' Feed or Reels tab, or on the profile grid, unless shared later.
- Metrics after about 24 hours; option to auto-share to followers if it performs well based on views in the first 72 hours.

### Art direction implication
- Optimize Reels for **sends** (useful, relatable, "send to someone who") and **watch time**; likes matter mainly for followers.
- Never repost TikToks with watermarks or reuse other creators' clips without transformation; for multi-location dealer groups, do not post the identical Reel to 20 store accounts. Make location-specific versions with real local footage and voiceover, or the copies risk being treated as duplicates.
- Use trial reels to test new concepts on cold audiences before committing the main account.

## Meta Advantage+ and Andromeda: creative as targeting

### What Meta says [primary]
- **Andromeda** (Engineering at Meta, 2024-12-02): Meta's ads **retrieval** engine, the stage that narrows tens of millions of ad candidates to a few thousand for ranking. Reported +6% recall in retrieval and +8% ads quality on selected segments; built for "exponential ad creatives growth from Advantage+ creative"; the post states advertisers who turned on Advantage+ creative AI targeting features saw a 22% ROAS increase, and image generation users an estimated +7% conversions [vendor claim]. Roadmap: "a more diverse set of ad candidates. Increased ad diversity can improve people's experience with ads and drive better advertiser outcomes."
- **Creative diversification** (Meta for Business, "The Creative Advantage", 2025): "a wide range of ad creatives with different themes, messages, and visuals to cater to diverse audience segments".
- **"Demystifying Creative Diversification"** (Meta for Business, 2025-12-16): "Creative diversification and creative iteration are two separate concepts." True diversification means "distinct campaign assets tailored to different personas or use cases". Per secondary readings of the post, Meta's system groups ads that share key visual and thematic attributes and treats look-alikes as variations of one creative, sharing learning and delivery at that level; Meta's example: identical visuals with different text CTAs count as the same creative [secondary: Riffkit and Roashack summaries; verify wording on the Meta page before quoting].
- Dimensions Meta lists for diversity (as summarized from the December 2025 post and Blueprint): personas and use cases, format, messaging approach (emotional vs features), visual, text and storyline differences, creative themes (humor, nostalgia, savings) [secondary summary of primary].
- **No published numbers**: Meta has not published a similarity threshold or a recommended number of concepts per ad set [secondary, consistent across independent summaries]. Any "Meta wants 10 to 20 creatives" figure is agency lore.

### Art direction implication: how many variants for Meta
- Count **concepts**, not files. A concept = distinct persona or use case + motivator + visual world + format (+ messenger).
- Practical starting portfolio per always-on prospecting campaign for a dealer group (Bright Matter planning heuristic, not a Meta rule): 4 to 6 distinct concepts, each produced in 2 to 3 formats (Reel 9:16, feed 4:5, static or carousel) with 2 to 3 hook variants. That is roughly 15 to 50 assets, but only 4 to 6 "bets" in Meta's eyes.
- Example concept set for a Ford store: (1) new-driver parent, safety tech, warm narrative, Reel; (2) contractor, F-150 payload, proof demo, creator-led walkaround; (3) commuter, hybrid fuel savings, calculator static; (4) lease-end, trade-in value anxiety, salesperson-to-camera; (5) local pride, community event, UGC montage.
- Resizes and headline swaps are not new concepts. Replace fatigued concepts with new concepts, not re-crops.

## YouTube: Shorts and long form

### Official statements
- Todd Beaupré, Senior Director of Growth and Discovery, with Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie (January 2025) [primary video, secondary summaries]: recommendations are **"pulled" for each viewer** when they open the app, using history, device, time of day and context; "Don't think algorithm, think audience"; YouTube optimizes for **satisfaction**, measured through likes, dislikes, survey responses and other signals, not only watch time or CTR; large language models now help YouTube understand video content more richly.
- Earlier Creator Insider interview with Beaupré: YouTube "run[s] surveys about recommendations about particular videos ... collect[s] millions of responses ... and feed[s] them right into the recommendation system"; "not all watchtime is created equal" [primary video transcript].
- Format strategy (Beaupré): "same audience, same channel"; putting Shorts, long form and podcasts on one channel helps the system connect a viewer across formats [primary].
- **Shorts view counting** (effective 2025-03-31): a view now counts each time a Short starts or replays, with no minimum watch time; the old metric is now **engaged views** in Advanced Mode; YPP eligibility and revenue still use engaged views [primary via TechCrunch and YouTube Community announcement].
- July 2025: YouTube clarified its YPP policy on mass-produced and repetitive ("inauthentic") content [secondary: TechCrunch, Search Engine Journal].

### Art direction implication
For Shorts, judge creative on engaged views and retention, not inflated raw views. For long form, the thumbnail and title must earn the click and the content must satisfy, because surveys and dislikes feed back. Dealer channels should mix Shorts (discovery) with longer walkarounds and comparison videos (evaluation), on one channel.

## LinkedIn

### Official statements [primary: LinkedIn Engineering]
- 2026 feed rearchitecture ("Engineering the next generation of LinkedIn's Feed"): unified **LLM-based retrieval** that understands what a post is about and how it relates to a member's evolving interests and career goals, plus a **Generative Recommender** sequential ranking model that reads more than a thousand of a member's past interactions. Signals include profile information (industry, skills, geography) and engagement over time: "what you've read, liked, commented on, returned back to, or simply scrolled past". The ranker predicts passive actions (click, skip, long dwell) and active actions (like, comment, share). LinkedIn states ranking relies on professional signals and engagement patterns, "never demographic attributes".
- AAAI 2026 paper (Ramanujam et al.): retrieval fine-tunes Meta's LLaMA 3 as a dual encoder; about 2,000 candidates retrieved from several million; significant gains for newer members [peer-reviewed conference].
- arXiv 2026 (Feed SR): the transformer ranker improved time spent by 2.10% in A/B tests; dwell-time popularity buckets remain important features [primary preprint].
- Older engineering post on dwell time: LinkedIn added a P(skip) model, treating short dwell as a negative signal, after finding updates viewed below a threshold were rarely engaged [primary].

### Art direction implication
On LinkedIn, the system reads the text of the post and the author's profile. Write posts whose topic is unmistakable in the first lines, make them worth stopping on (dwell), and invite substantive comments. For dealer groups and Bright Matter itself: employer brand, leadership and community stories; for B2B clients, buying-group-friendly proof.

## Pinterest

### Official statements [primary: Pinterest Engineering blog]
- Home feed is personalized in stages: retrieval of candidates based on user interests, pre-ranking, then ranking with the "Pinnability" model, which predicts personalized relevance from Pin, user and context signals.
- **Saves (repins)** are "one of the most important user actions" and the main proxy for engagement; hides are a key negative signal (TransAct, updated 2025).
- TransActV2 (2025-06): models up to 16,000 lifelong user actions; A/B test showed +6.35% repins, 12.8% fewer hides, +1.41% time spent [primary, platform-reported].
- Pinterest says it serves more than 570 million monthly active users (2025 engineering post) [primary].

### Art direction implication
Pinterest is a planning and saving surface: design Pins as idea references people want to keep (checklists, comparisons, "before you buy" guides, seasonal maintenance). Vertical, legible, evergreen. Strong fit for service departments and lifestyle angles, weaker for price-led offers.

## X

### Official statements [primary: xai-org/x-algorithm GitHub repository, README, 2026 versions]
- The For You feed combines in-network posts (Thunder) and out-of-network posts (Phoenix retrieval, SimClusters), ranked by **Phoenix**, a transformer (originally ported from Grok-1) that predicts probabilities of many actions: favorite, reply, repost, quote, share, share via DM, share via copy link, clicks, video quality view, dwell, follow author, and negatives (not interested, mute, block, report, not dwelled).
- Final score = sum of weight times predicted probability; positive actions positive weights, negative actions negative weights; weights live in the code.
- Adjustments: **author diversity** decay for repeated posts from one author; **out-of-network discount** below 1; **new-author boost** for low-impression authors; a re-ranker trades a little score for less similarity between neighbors.
- The README warns weights scale predicted probabilities, not raw counts.
- [flag] Third-party "deep dive" repos interpreting exact weights (for example claims that copy-link shares are worth many times a like, or that dwell carries zero weight) are interpretations of a fast-changing codebase; verify against the current `home-mixer/params` file before quoting.

### Art direction implication
On X, content people would forward privately or reply to thoughtfully wins; content that prompts mutes or reports is heavily penalized. Low priority for most dealer clients beyond customer service and local news moments.

## Snapchat

### Official statements [primary: Snapchat Support, "How We Rank Content on Spotlight"; Snap Creator Hub]
- Ranking predicts probabilities of actions such as favoriting or sharing, and expected viewing time.
- Highest-importance indicators for Spotlight include country, language, age and gender (for local and appropriate content), content and creators similar to what you viewed, favorited, subscribed to, shared or commented on, content category and metadata (view time, creator, location, hashtags), and negative signals (hide, report).
- Creator Hub: time spent watching, favorites and shares with friends are positive; skipping quickly is negative.
- Content must meet stricter Content Guidelines to be recommended beyond friends; off-platform links are limited on Spotlight and Map [primary: Snap content recommendation guidelines].

### Art direction implication
Snapchat skews young; relevant for first-car buyers, used inventory and service promos aimed at students. Spotlight content must be native, vertical, quick, and should not lean on outbound links.

## Threads

### Official statements [primary: Meta Transparency Center, Threads Feed system card]
- Feed blends followed accounts and recommendations, ranked by predicted interest. Input signals listed include viewing time per post, likes, engagement rate with authors, reply-chain engagement (replies and replies to replies over the past hour and six hours), profile taps and follows.
- November 2024: Mosseri said Threads was "rebalancing ranking to prioritize content from people you follow" [secondary: Mashable, quoting Mosseri's Threads post].
- September 2025: Threads tested a prototype letting users "tag" an account to steer their algorithm (not public at the time) [secondary: TechCrunch].

### Art direction implication
Threads rewards conversation. Use it for founder and brand voice, quick takes and replies, not repurposed ad creative.

## Ad automation platforms and variant counts

### Meta Advantage+ (see Andromeda above)
- Variant guidance: concepts over files; no official number.
- Meta Business Help Center lists a limit of 50 ads per ad set [secondary summary citing Meta help; verify current limit].

### TikTok Smart+ [primary: TikTok Ads help and Smart+ guide]
- Upgraded Smart+ (2025 to 2026) unifies manual and automated flows: full, partial or manual automation; per TikTok's 2026 blog, up to 30 ad groups per campaign, 30 asset groups per ad group and 50 creatives per asset group.
- TikTok's Smart+ Web Campaigns guide: "Add at least six creative assets during campaign creation" [primary].
- TikTok SMB Creative Playbook 2025: Smart Creative refreshes stale ads; "If you have 5+ creatives, Smart Creative can help uplift ad performance by up to 9%" [vendor claim].
- Symphony Automation inside Smart+: Recommended Creatives (can generate TikTok-ready videos from a product URL; Auto-add refreshes creatives mid-flight) and Automatic Enhancements (resize, quality, hooks, translation, dubbing) [primary].

### Google Performance Max [primary: Google Ads Help]
- Per asset group, "Add as many assets as possible, including up to 15 headlines, 5 descriptions, 20 images in different orientations, and 5 videos in different orientations" (best-practice page); spec pages list up to 15 videos across horizontal, square and vertical [primary; Google's own pages differ on the video maximum, check the current spec].
- Recommended minimums: 11+ headlines (3 to 15), 2+ long headlines (1 to 5), 4+ descriptions (2 to 5), 4+ landscape and 4+ square images, 2+ vertical 4:5 images, logos, at least one video per orientation.
- "Excellent" Ad Strength requires at least 6 sitelinks, your own videos in horizontal, square and vertical (not auto-generated), and all 15 headline and 5 description slots [primary].
- If no video is uploaded, Google may auto-generate one from your assets [primary].
- Google recommends waiting 2 to 3 weeks before replacing low performers, and separating asset groups by theme, category or audience [primary].

### Google Demand Gen [primary: Google Ads Help]
- Up to 20 images per ad (square, horizontal 1.91:1, vertical 4:5, vertical 9:16), "upload three of each aspect orientation"; up to five headlines (40 characters) and five descriptions; one to five videos; videos under 10 seconds will not serve in YouTube in-stream; Google suggests videos over 15 seconds to maximize reach; 9:16 recommended for Shorts.

### Art direction implication: production math per platform

| Platform | Unit of diversity | Minimum credible kit per campaign (Bright Matter heuristic) |
|---|---|---|
| Meta Advantage+ | Concept (persona, motivator, visual world, format) | 4 to 6 concepts x 2 to 3 formats x 2 to 3 hooks |
| TikTok Smart+ | Video creative | 6+ native vertical videos (TikTok's stated minimum), refreshed every few weeks |
| Google PMax | Asset group per theme | Per asset group: 15 headlines, 5 descriptions, about 15 to 20 images across 3 ratios, 3 custom videos (16:9, 1:1, 9:16) |
| Google Demand Gen | Ad | 3 images per ratio, 3+ headlines, 1 to 5 videos including 9:16 |
| Organic Reels / TikTok / Shorts | Post | Original per account, per location; no cross-posting identical clips across store accounts |

Supported by platform documentation for the minimums; the concept counts are an operating heuristic. Measure creative fatigue in-platform and refresh by concept.

## Hook and retention science for short-form

### What the evidence says
- **Speed of recognition**: Meta's Facebook IQ, citing Fors Marsh Group tests, says people can recall mobile feed content at a statistically significant rate after 0.25 seconds of exposure [vendor claim, commissioned].
- **Partial views carry value**: a Nielsen analysis of 173 BrandEffect studies for Facebook found viewers who watched under three seconds generated up to 47% of total campaign value, and under ten seconds up to 74%, depending on metric [vendor claim, platform-commissioned].
- [flag] A Meta creative-tips page repeats the "goldfish attention span" claim (8 seconds vs 9 for a goldfish), a widely debunked figure with no traceable study. Distrust that page's other unsourced figures.
- [flag] A widely circulated stat that "63% of TikTok's top ads deliver the key message in the first 3 seconds" could not be traced to a primary TikTok page in this research pass. Do not quote it.
- **Branding timing, peer-reviewed**: Teixeira, Wedel and Pieters, "Moment-to-Moment Optimal Branding in TV Commercials: Preventing Avoidance by Pulsing" (*Marketing Science*, 2010): heavy, early brand presence increases zapping; brief, repeated brand "pulses" reduce avoidance while preserving brand memory [peer-reviewed].
- **Emotion and retention, peer-reviewed**: Teixeira, Wedel and Pieters, "Emotion-Induced Engagement in Internet Video Advertisements" (*Journal of Marketing Research*, 2012): joy and surprise expressed by viewers predicted concentrated attention and lower abandonment [peer-reviewed].
- **Attention and memory**: Nelson-Field/Amplified: memory encoding begins around 1.5 to 3 seconds of active attention, and well-branded assets make short exposures count (file 06) [vendor claim].
- **Platform signals that reward retention**: TikTok weights completion of longer videos; Instagram's top signal list starts with watch time; YouTube Shorts revenue and eligibility use engaged views; Snapchat penalizes quick skips; LinkedIn penalizes short dwell (P(skip)) [primary, above].

### The 3-second view, watch time, saves and shares
- The "3-second view" is a Meta ads metric and a common hook-rate denominator; it is a reporting convention, not a scientific threshold.
- Shares and sends predict reach to non-followers (Instagram) and appear as explicit predicted actions on X and Snapchat [primary].
- Saves are Pinterest's core engagement proxy [primary] and appear in Meta ranking predictions; on Instagram, Mosseri's 2025 top three did not include saves.

### Art direction implication: a short-form build checklist
1. Frame 1 must work as a still: face or product in motion, high contrast, brand color present, a readable line of text.
2. State the premise in the first 1 to 2 seconds; deliver a payoff before 10 seconds; give a reason to stay to the end (reveal, countdown, before and after).
3. Pulse the brand (color, sonic tag, character, logo) two or three times rather than one heavy opening logo.
4. Design a "send" moment: a line or visual the viewer wants to show one specific friend.
5. Captions burned in, keywords spoken and written for search.
6. Loop-friendly endings where the platform counts replays.

## Creator-led and UGC trends

### What platforms are building [primary]
- TikTok: Creator Marketplace (800K+ creators per TikTok's 2025 SMB playbook) and Affiliate Creatives for Ads, which lets TikTok Shop merchants use authorized affiliate content in GMV Max campaigns (TikTok World 2025).
- Meta: partnership ads (cited by Meta as a distinct format in diversification guidance) [secondary summary of primary].
- Instagram 2024 to 2025: ranking inputs to help smaller and original creators [primary].

### Evidence and status
- Platforms consistently promote creator content; independent, peer-reviewed evidence that UGC outperforms brand-made ads in general is limited. Performance claims (for example, "authentic content wins") are mostly vendor or agency claims. Meta's diversification guidance supports creators as a source of **distinct messengers and visual worlds**, which is the defensible reason to use them.

### Art direction implication
Use creators and real staff to add distinct faces, voices and settings to the concept portfolio, not as a style fad. For dealer groups, the strongest "creators" are often real salespeople and technicians on camera, filmed locally per store, which also satisfies originality rules.

## Summary: what changes for the art director

1. Creative is the targeting input on Meta, TikTok and Google: plan portfolios of distinct concepts.
2. Every platform punishes duplicates and reposts: produce original, location-specific cuts.
3. Retention, sends and saves beat likes for reach; design for them explicitly.
4. Buyers decide before contact (B2B and auto): brand memory and evaluation-stage assets (reviews, VDPs, GBP, AI-readable facts) carry the sale.
5. Search is now TikTok, YouTube, Google AI answers and ChatGPT: say and show the facts plainly with numbers and sources.

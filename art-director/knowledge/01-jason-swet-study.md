## Core teachings, in his words and structure

### 1. Idea sizing (scale, not quality)

From "What even is a BIG IDEA" (Feb 2026) and the big idea triangle videos (Jun 2026).

- Ideas sit on a scale from small to big: **tactics** (no real idea), **one-offs** (an idea that can only be one thing), **executions** (one expression of a larger idea), **campaigns** and **platforms** (big ideas; a platform is big enough to span years or inspire multiple campaigns).
- The x-axis is level of detail. Executions need every detail ("a Nike shoe built from flowers in Times Square"). Big ideas need almost none: "Big ideas should be inspired by a single insight articulated so concise that you can write it on a post-it note." Examples he cites: Snickers "You're not you when you're hungry", Nike "Winning isn't for everyone."
- "To think bigger, you often need to think smaller."
- Visual art directors struggle with concepts "because our visual brains jump straight to execution."

### 2. The big idea triangle: Insight, Idea, Execution

- **Insight** (the point of the triangle, smallest): "one very simple focused human truth that people aren't talking about yet." Formula: "People think X, but the reality is Y." An insight is a revelation, not a tagline. "We're not going to dress it up yet."
- **Idea**: what you DO with the insight. "Big ideas need verbs." Verbs he lists: dramatize, contrast, flip, rip up. "The do is the verb. Is there something you're actually doing with this campaign?"
- **Execution** (widest, most detail): print, out of home, social, stunt, app, product innovation. Every execution "ladders back" and "follows the same formula."
- Umbrella version: the insight is the tip ("without this you'll have a gaping hole"), the idea is the canopy, executions are the ribs.
- Worked example, Nike World Cup 2026 "Rip the Script": insight is that football feels increasingly planned and staged while its most beautiful moments are instinct. Idea is take football's script and rip it up. Executions: hero film as rallying cry, athlete vignettes each ripping their own script, some touchpoints only borrowing look and feel. "A great idea statement also starts to hint at the look and feel."
- He credits the insight-first lineage to David Ogilvy "and beyond."

### 3. The Language of Art Direction

- Copywriters bring the concept to life through words; art directors through **visual language**: color, imagery, styling, visual devices, look and feel.
- "The big unlock": define the visual language by also **writing it down**. His CDs made him add a short write-up to every mood board. Writing "removes the subjectivity that comes from looking at references and articulates clear boundaries." Without it: "mismatched luggage."
- **Metaphor (Ep. 2)**: describe how it should feel by colliding existing visual worlds. Templates:
  - "It should feel like ___ but ___."
  - "It's like ___ meets ___."
  - "Imagine ___ but ___."
  - His examples: Wes Anderson but shot on a flip phone; a luxury ad made in a garage; a horror movie in pastel tones with soft lighting.
- "All creative concepts really are" combinations: "a Venn diagram of where the product meets a relatable human insight."

### 4. The Theory of Taste (2026 series)

His central metaphor: taste is food. He pushes it as far as it will go.

- **Ep. 1, Voracious eating (inputs).** "No one trusts the taste of someone who only consumes fast food." Eat widely: other genres, non-mainstream ingredients, sources that are hard to reach, "a virtual garden properly gatekept from the algorithms, an internet archive trip." "Replace scrolling with digging, replace escape with wandering." But "taste alone does not give you an ability to cook," and taste "definitely won't be the moat everyone thinks it is."
- **Ep. 2, Articulation (outputs).** "Your taste is useless if you can't articulate it." Three levels:
  1. Express it: save what you like (he uses Cosmos collections via the phone share sheet).
  2. Feelings and metaphor: "this feels uncomfortable" (a collection titled *uncomfy*); "feels like a Michel Gondry movie." This level is for non-technical clients.
  3. Ingredients: the specific technique ("hand-drawn mixed media", "the flash lighting"). Name collections after ingredients: "I'm filling my pantry." This level is for directing vendors and collaborators in their own language.
- **Ep. 3, Visual fluency.** Fluency comes from immersion: "The best chefs don't just like sushi, they moved to Japan." Go to the source: find who represents the photographer (artist and director rep agencies) and you find a roster the algorithm never surfaces. "Your mood board can't just be inspiration. You have to know that you can execute. Know who can execute."
- **Ep. 4, The spectrum of tastes.** Taste is not binary. Types:
  - **Mainstream taste**: widest net, safe, undervalued, "can unlock scale."
  - **Cultural taste**: "gatekept by lived experience." You can study, serve, and amplify it but "you can't claim it if you didn't live it." The hardest for brands.
  - **Countertaste**: conviction to reject what everyone wants; tends to form a collective, then gets absorbed by mainstream or institutionalized by the elite.
  - **Elite taste**: criteria gatekept by access or privilege; mainstream envies it.
  - **Commercial taste**: the CD's sense for what fits business objectives. "A little flavor of aspiration mixed with something familiar and inviting."
  - "Everyone loves a burger, but even burgers can't be served every meal."
- Ep. 5 (teased): the senses beyond taste.

### 5. Named visual techniques (his growing toolbox)

- **Figure/ground reversal**: when the visual is too obvious, "turn the subtraction into the subject."
- **Genre quotation / visual quotation / pastiche**: when visuals don't mean anything, borrow the codes of a known genre, era, format, or specific work. The borrowed association does the talking and lets viewers predict what comes next.
- **Form substitution**: when too literal, "use the shape of one thing to mean something else."
- **Visible facture**: facture is how a thing was made; show the process (paint strokes, the pen line, handmade props). Seen in Szilveszter Makó's painterly photography and Polaroid's ballpoint campaign.
- **Hockney joiner (panography)**: Hockney disliked that a photo freezes one instant; the eye takes hundreds of micro-snapshots over time. Use it to collapse time into one frame: sports motion, attention or distraction, accumulation, time-lapse ideas. Always "trace back the visual lineage."
- **Contrast** (Rosalía, "Berghain", by production company Canada, director Nicolás Méndez): "bringing clearly disparate subjects together." An orchestra crammed into mundane spaces but lit naturally: "High art shoved into the mundane." Contrast can be of subjects and situations, not only color or scale.
- **Reframe the familiar**: take something recognized and break the pattern (his read of the 4x4 image-grid trend).
- **Think 2D / work within the frame**: art direct the shot, not the whole room. Interior designers break rooms into moments; content only needs what is inside the frame.
- **Signifier rationale**: Polaroid's blue ballpoint pen. "You can say a thing, but it's how you say it." Always have a rationale for stakeholders: blue ink historically signifies authenticity (you sign contracts in blue; it's the family note pen).

### 6. Hooks are older than TikTok ("The Lost Art of the Hook")

- A hook "grabs and holds your attention. It needs to hold." Holding is what separates a hook from clickbait.
- Examples across media: first line of *The Hunger Games* and *1984*; the cold opens of *Breaking Bad* (flying pants) and *The Dark Knight*; music hooks now moved to the top of songs in the streaming era; book covers; newspaper headlines; campaign names (Dumb Ways to Die, #LikeAGirl, Shot on iPhone, The Man Your Man Could Smell Like).
- Hooks can be visual, audio, text, experiential, even smell and touch.
- Characteristics he looks for: sparks curiosity and questions; **reframes something familiar**, ideally flips it counterintuitively; **instantly understandable** (clarity); **hits a nerve** (emotional, humor; not necessarily provocative).
- Social is "more like fishing than sitting in a theater." Images are lures.

### 7. Roles: CD vs AD vs designer

- **Creative director**: sets vision and creative strategy; the "creative compass"; approves concepts and anything that goes out the door; big-picture brand alignment; pitches and sells to clients. Can come from any discipline.
- **Art director**: under the CD, more hands-on, leads the visual direction and look and feel, often paired with a copywriter, must be multi-disciplinary and good enough to execute alone. Not necessarily senior to the specialists they direct.
- **AD vs designer**: Sir John Hegarty's framing, per Jason: the designer is "the architect of clarity," the art director "a creative anarchist" who provokes. Jason's universal differentiator: the art director is a **visual coordinator**, earlier in the pipeline and closer to the client, who decides how one concept is executed across channels and coordinates the specialists (photographers, directors, designers, animators), or wears those hats when there's no budget.
- "The best art directors I know are trying to become anything but an art director": photographer, editor, set designer, producer, illustrator. The AD is the "visual orchestrator" and must be "multilingual."
- Career advice: don't start in creative direction. Start working FOR a creative director in a role with one hard skill.

### 8. Brand and culture case studies he teaches from

- **Gap (2025-2026)**: music videos, not ads (Miko, Katseye, Tyla), director Bethany Vargas. Three lessons: (1) fashion as entertainment, a platform not a one-off; "a brand's unique taste is refined from consistent decisions over time"; (2) **movement is the product demo**, no voiceover with price and features; (3) creating culture means **enabling** already-flourishing artist worlds with resources, not casting or hijacking them. Brands that start with "how do we capitalize on Latin music" make terrible work.
- **Jon Bellion**: six years dark, then a blurry screenshot of a URL with no caption or link, a 14-minute unpausable video, a lo-fi private forum, WeTransfer links of unreleased songs with "chop it up, post it, no takedowns." "We've optimized everything to be instant, but when something actually matters, maybe it should take a little effort." "You don't build cults with reach. You build them with ritual."
- **Oatly x Nikolas Bentel, Matcha Urgency Case** (agency Small World, Dan Salkey): treat creators as **creatives, not influencers** (bring them in upstream on briefs); treat influencer budgets as **creative and production budgets, not media spend**. Cultural shift: "absurd levels of energy into small, silly ideas."
- **The Ordinary, "Periodic Fable"** (Uncommon): a dystopian classroom indexing every misleading beauty term; every movement is a real skincare routine gesture gathered from TikTok. Truth is why it resonates. Built to look unlike anything else in the feed.
- **Coinbase, "Your Way Out of Their System"** (Isle of Any, director Oscar Hudson): live action that looks like a video game: textures printed onto suits, pixel-printed sets, actors trained in game movement logic. Analog human craft to counter a brand that feels inhuman. His question: can creative direction alone change how you feel about a brand?
- **Skylrk (Justin Bieber)**: the ghostly gradient imagery comes from studio Serifa, which had been making that style since 2021. Lesson: a brand's image "doesn't need to be built from scratch"; commission the artists whose existing language fits. Commercial image makers are like ghostwriters with no end credits.
- **Polaroid** campaign: simple, "not all that original," but one ingredient (blue ballpoint handwriting) proves the value of art direction.
- **LA28 emblem**: low-concept campaigns "rely so heavily on the craft that if a single design falls flat, it can pull the rest down." Compares to Patreon's 3D-led rebrand.
- **Donald Glover's Gilga**: non-traditional roles (taste/acquisitions, conversational marketing, AI prompt animator, AI prompt engineer and librarian, concept head, architect). Glover on *This Is America*: "culture is just compression of information"; "the internet kind of robs moments," so build a moment that could only be happening right now.
- **Creators vs the algorithm** (Casey Neistat, GxAce, Kraig Adams): "algorithms don't tell you how to make valuable content. They just try to find an audience for it."
- **Canoopsy**: creator who branded himself like "90s Apple"; aesthetic craft builds a following that watches for him, not just the topic, and makes him a natural brand collaborator.
- Spaces: art direct your home "like the movies"; pop-ups and retail (Post Malone's Raising Cane's, Lonely Ghost grocery store).

### 9. Process and craft habits

- **Spec campaign recipe** (his Oakley "delusion" project): 1) concept = insight + idea + visual direction (insight: we call ambitious people delusional when they see their potential most clearly; idea: reframe distortion as clear vision; visual: in-camera prism lens distortion with delusionally confident poses); 2) recruit friends; 3) book a studio (Peerspace); 4) shoot; 5) design (hand images and references to a designer and let them run); 6) mock up in situ. "Sometimes you just got to embrace the constraints."
- **Mock up exactly what's in your head** so the client sees the vision; that's how the craziest ideas get greenlit.
- **The lazy creative**: spend less time on what doesn't matter; CoolText or PhotoFunia beats opening Photoshop for a deck that just needs to sell the idea.
- **Firefly Boards / one board from brief to execution**: his whole process lives on a single board.
- Make thing, put it out, repeat. Don't overthink.

### 10. Industry outlook he subscribes to

- Holding company consolidation (Omnicom absorbing IPG) and layoffs. Quotes Brian Collins ("Creativity is not dying. What is dying is old, broken industry models") and Zoe Scaman ("the industry is not disappearing, it's disseminating" into in-house teams, collectives, direct brand relationships).
- Art direction now belongs in marketing broadly and the creator economy, not only agencies. This is Bright Matter's positioning in a sentence.

## How the agent should use this study

1. Default concept workflow: **Insight ("people think X, but really Y") → Idea (a verb) → Look and feel (metaphor + written boundaries) → Executions**, then size the idea on the Idea Sizing scale and say whether it's a tactic, one-off, execution, campaign, or platform.
2. Always write the mood board down: feelings, a metaphor template, then named ingredients, then who can execute.
3. When a concept is weak, run his diagnostics: too obvious → figure/ground reversal; meaningless → genre quotation; too literal → form substitution; flat → contrast of subjects; forgettable → reframe the familiar; generic → visible facture.
4. Trace visual lineage for every reference (who did this first, what DNA does it share).
5. Ask "whose taste?" (mainstream, cultural, counter, elite, commercial) before judging any direction. Respect that cultural taste is earned by lived experience.
6. Treat creators as creatives upstream; prefer ritual over reach for community; prefer enabling culture over hijacking it.

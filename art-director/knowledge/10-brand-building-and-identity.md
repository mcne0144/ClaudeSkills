# 10. Brand Building and Identity

How brands are defined, named, drawn, launched, and sold, plus the campaigns that prove the craft. Written for an art-direction agent working with Bright Matter (branding, ad campaigns, social, websites). Use it to diagnose a brief, pressure-test a platform, choose an identity approach, and cite precedent with the right caveats.

**How to read the sourcing.** Every factual claim names its source inline with a tier:
- **T1** = primary (the brand, agency, award body, standards body, or author's own site)
- **T2** = reputable trade or news press (Ad Age, Adweek, Campaign, Design Week, Creative Review, The Guardian, NYT, BBC, Bloomberg, Fortune)
- **T3** = secondary or social (Wikipedia, LinkedIn posts, aggregators, blogs)
- **Canon** = widely documented industry history that was not re-fetched in this research session. Treat as reliable but re-check before quoting a number to a client.
- Maker or sponsor performance figures are labeled **(claim)**. They come from award entries and agency case studies, which are written to win.

Research date: 2026-10-03.

## 1. The brand platform: anatomy

A brand platform is the small set of decisions that every later decision (name, logo, campaign, caption) must obey. Agencies label the parts differently. These are the parts that matter and the test for each.

| Part | What it answers | Test of a good one | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Why the company exists beyond profit | Would a competitor be embarrassed to copy it? Does it change a decision? | Generic virtue ("empower people") |
| **Positioning** | What you are, for whom, against what alternative | Names a competitive alternative and a frame of reference | Lists features, no enemy |
| **Promise** | What the customer can count on every time | Could be broken (so it means something) | Unfalsifiable ("quality") |
| **Values** | How the company behaves when it costs something | Each value implies a trade-off you will accept | Five nouns nobody remembers |
| **Personality** | If the brand were a person, who | Specific enough to cast an actor | "Friendly, approachable, innovative" |
| **Voice** | How the personality sounds in words | You can rewrite a sentence "wrong" and "right" | No examples, only adjectives |
| **Proposition** | The single most motivating thing to say now | One sentence, one idea | Three ideas joined by "and" |
| **Reason to believe (RTB)** | Proof that makes the proposition credible | Verifiable, ideally visible | Restates the claim |

**Working rules for the agent**
- Purpose and positioning are slow-moving. Proposition and RTB can change per campaign.
- Personality and voice are where art direction lives. Translate adjectives into **visual behaviors**: "irreverent" becomes "breaks the grid on purpose, uses one unexpected color, lets the logo be the punchline."
- If a client cannot pick a competitive alternative, positioning is not done. Do not start the identity.
- Write the platform so a 20-year-old social manager could use it on a Tuesday. Eugene Healey makes this argument directly: "A beautiful positioning line means nothing unless it can be articulated by a 20 year old undergrad student" (Healey interview, Post-Culture by Sibling Studio Substack, 2026-03-20, T3).

## 2. Positioning methods

### Ries and Trout: Positioning (Canon)
- Al Ries and Jack Trout, *Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind* (book 1981; the idea first ran as articles in *Industrial Marketing* in 1972). Canon.
- Core claim: positioning happens in the prospect's mind, not in the product. Own one word or one rung of a mental ladder. Be first in a category, or create a new category where you can be first.
- Classic illustration: Avis "We're number two. We try harder." (DDB, 1962). Canon.
- **Transferable principle:** A brand that tries to own every attribute owns none. Pick the word.

### April Dunford: Obviously Awesome (T1/T3)
- *Obviously Awesome: How to Nail Product Positioning so Customers Get It, Buy It, Love It* (2019; review dated 2019-05-10, Editors Reads, T3).
- Five (plus one) components, in this order (summarized by Nat Eliason's notes and Heinz Marketing 2023-09-27, T3):
  1. **Competitive alternatives** (what customers would do if you did not exist, including "a spreadsheet" or "hire an intern")
  2. **Unique attributes** (what you have that alternatives lack)
  3. **Value and proof** (what those attributes enable)
  4. **Target market characteristics** (who cares most about that value)
  5. **Market category** (the frame that makes the value obvious)
  6. Bonus: **relevant trends**
- Three category strategies: Head to Head, Big Fish Small Pond, Create a New Game (Eliason notes, T3).
- Key move: choose the category **last**, derived from proven value, not first from aspiration (Editors Reads, T3).
- **Transferable principle:** The category label does half your marketing. Choose one whose assumptions work for you.

### Marty Neumeier: The Brand Gap and Zag (Canon)
- *The Brand Gap* (2003): a brand is "a person's gut feeling about a product, service, or company." It is defined by customers, not companies. The "gap" is between strategy (logic) and creativity (magic). Canon.
- *Zag* (2006): when everyone zigs, zag. Radical differentiation. Canon.
- **The onliness statement** (Zag): "Our [brand] is the ONLY [category] that [differentiator]." Neumeier's fuller version adds who, where, why, and when. If you cannot finish the sentence with "only," you are not differentiated. Canon.
- **Transferable principle:** Run every positioning draft through the onliness test before any design starts.

### Byron Sharp and Jenni Romaniuk (Ehrenberg-Bass) (Canon)
- Sharp, *How Brands Grow* (2010); Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (2018). Canon.
- Counterweight to differentiation obsession: growth comes mostly from **mental availability** (being easily thought of in buying situations) and **physical availability**. Distinctiveness (being recognizable) often matters more than differentiation (being meaningfully different). Canon.
- Eugene Healey cites this lineage ("the Scaman's and Sharp's, the Binet's, the Roach's and Ritson's") as the classical canon creators should learn (Healey LinkedIn, 2026-07, T3).
- **Transferable principle for art direction:** Your job is to build assets that are instantly recognizable and used relentlessly, not to reinvent the look every campaign.

### Mark Pollard: The Four Points (T1/T2)
- Pollard (Mighty Jungle, Sweathead) teaches **Problem, Insight, Advantage, Strategy** (Pollard on JustCreative podcast, 2024-11-20, T2/T3).
  - Problem: "an obstacle or a barrier that's in the way of someone buying something or doing something"
  - Insight: "an unspoken human truth that sheds new light on the problem" (joining phrase: "But here's the thing about...", Sweathead lesson 29, T1)
  - Advantage: what is unique and motivating about the brand to people
  - Strategy: a statement "typically in the form of show that X is Y," which solves the problem by combining insight and advantage
- Process: list five problems, pick one, ask "What's causing this?" five times, then write the four sentences. Draft five to ten sets, then write three 200-word "strategy stories" (JustCreative, T2/T3; Sweathead lesson 29, T1).
- The four sentences double as a presentation spine: problem slides, insight slides, advantage, then reveal the work (Pollard, JustCreative, T2/T3).

## 3. Brand architecture

| Model | Definition | Examples (Canon unless noted) | Choose it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Branded house** | One master brand, descriptive sub-brands | Google (Maps, Drive), FedEx (Express, Ground) | Trust transfers, one story, lower cost |
| **House of brands** | Independent brands, parent hidden | P&G (Tide, Pampers), Unilever (Dove, Axe) | Segments conflict, risk must be isolated |
| **Endorsed** | Sub-brands with their own identity plus a visible parent endorsement | Marriott's "Courtyard by Marriott" | New brand needs borrowed trust but its own personality |
| **Hybrid** | Mix of the above | Most large companies in practice | Acquisitions and legacy |

**Recent case, Amazon by Koto (T1/T2):** An 18-month project across 15 markets and 50+ sub-brands. Koto grouped sub-brands into **cohorts** (entertainment, health, devices, grocery), each with shared visual rules, all linked to the master brand. It unified "the many oranges" into one Smile Orange, redrew the wordmark, built a custom alphabet (Amazon Logo Sans), and evolved the Kindle-born Ember typeface into Ember Modern with NaN foundry, supporting 364 languages (Koto project page, T1; Design Week 2025-04-29, T2; Creative Review 2025-04-29, T2). First logo update in over 20 years; the previous mark was by Turner Duckworth (Design Week, T2). Brand strategy groundwork was by Sylvain (Creative Review, T2).
- **Transferable principle:** For a multi-location or multi-product Bright Matter client, define **fixed points** (logo, master color, type) and **flex points** (secondary palette, imagery tone) per cohort. Architecture is a design system decision as much as a naming one.

## 4. Naming

### Name types (spectrum from literal to abstract)
| Type | What it is | Examples (Canon) | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive** | Says what it does | General Motors, The Weather Channel | Clear, weak to trademark, hard to stretch |
| **Suggestive** | Hints at a benefit | Pinterest, Netflix | Balance of meaning and ownability |
| **Abstract / arbitrary** | Real word, unrelated meaning | Apple (computers), Amazon | Strong mark, needs investment to fill with meaning |
| **Invented / coined** | New word | Kodak, Xerox, Häagen-Dazs | Strongest legal protection, zero built-in meaning |
| **Compound** | Two words fused | Facebook, YouTube, Snapchat | Memorable, domain-friendly |
| **Metaphor** | Borrowed image or story | Nike (goddess), Patagonia, Oracle | Emotional depth, can feel grand |
| **Founder / acronym** | Person or initials | Ford, IBM, H&M | Heritage; acronyms are hard to love |

Recent real examples from Tanj (Scott Milano's naming studio, T1/T3):
- **Actual** (renamed from code name BlinkReceipt, spun out of Microblink): a familiar English word repurposed. "Actual purchase data. Actual insights. Actual value." (Milano LinkedIn, 2026-09, T3)
- **Flute** (payments for SMBs): metaphor for effortlessness and harmony; identity by Focus Lab (Milano LinkedIn, 2026-06-24, T3)

### Process (practitioner synthesis)
1. **Brief**: positioning, personality, architecture role, markets and languages, must-avoid list, domain expectations.
2. **Territories**: 4 to 6 creative directions (e.g., "motion," "light," "craft," invented).
3. **Generate wide**: hundreds of candidates. Milano warns that AI tools return the same handful of names to millions of founders, so trademark searches are "waaaaay more crowded than ever" and novel names gain value (Milano LinkedIn, 2026-07-17, T3; he cites a NYT piece that nearly 60% of new founders used AI to launch, including naming, which is his paraphrase).
4. **Screen**: knockout trademark search, linguistic check in target languages, domain and handle check, pronunciation test.
5. **Shortlist** 5 to 10, present each **in situ** (on a storefront, app icon, receipt, in a sentence), never as a word on a white slide.
6. **Legal clearance** by counsel before announcing.

### Trademark screening basics (not legal advice; Canon)
- The US distinctiveness spectrum (from *Abercrombie & Fitch v. Hunting World*, 1976): generic (unprotectable), descriptive (needs secondary meaning), suggestive, arbitrary, fanciful (strongest). Canon.
- Marks are registered by class under the Nice Classification (45 classes). Conflict test is "likelihood of confusion": similar mark plus related goods or services. Canon.
- Agent behavior: run a free knockout check (USPTO search, EUIPO, Google, app stores, social handles) and **always** tell the user that full clearance requires a trademark attorney.

## 5. Identity systems

### Logo types
| Type | Description | Examples (Canon unless noted) | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wordmark** | Name set in distinctive type | Google, Coca-Cola, FedEx | Name is short and distinctive |
| **Lettermark / monogram** | Initials | IBM, HBO, CNN | Long name, established brand |
| **Pictorial** | Recognizable image | Apple, Twitter bird, Target | Need a symbol that travels without words |
| **Abstract** | Non-literal shape | Nike swoosh, Pepsi globe, Airbnb Bélo | Want an ownable shape you fill with meaning |
| **Emblem** | Name inside a badge or seal | Starbucks, Harley-Davidson, NFL | Heritage, institutional, apparel |
| **Combination** | Symbol plus wordmark | Burger King, Doritos | Default for new brands; can drop the word later |
| **Dynamic / generative** | Rule-based mark that changes | MIT Media Lab, Tate (Wolff Olins, 2000) | Brand's subject is variety or change |
| **Mascot / character** | A character as primary asset | Duolingo's Duo, KFC Colonel | Social-first, personality-led brands |

Notes with sources:
- **Airbnb Bélo (DesignStudio, 2014):** designed as "a symbol of belonging" and "a symbol that anyone can draw," with a "community symbol" variant users could adapt (James Greenfield, Design Week 2014-07-17, T2). It was widely mocked at launch and is now familiar (Paul Stafford, Creative Review 2024-10-17, T2). DesignStudio won after a pitch where it built an Airbnb "listing" in its own office (Design Week, T2).
- **Mastercard (Pentagram, Michael Bierut and Luke Hayman, 2016):** kept the 1968 interlocking circles, simplified them, lowercase wordmark. Pentagram reports 81% of consumers recognized the symbol without the name (Pentagram, T1). In January 2019 Mastercard dropped the name from the mark in select contexts, citing "more than 80 percent" spontaneous recognition (Mastercard press release, T1). Bierut: "all these wild explorations seemed to be ignoring an answer that was right in front of our noses" (Bierut essay "The Shock of the Familiar," Mastercard design center, July 2016, T1).
- **Transferable principle (Mastercard):** Equity first. Explore wildly, then ask what the brand already owns.

### Distinctive brand assets (DBAs)
- Romaniuk's framework (Canon): assets are scored on **fame** (what share of category buyers link it to you) and **uniqueness** (how few link it to competitors). Build, protect, or drop accordingly.
- Asset types: color, shape, logo, character, typeface, sound, tagline, pack shape, a recurring scene or device.
- **Proof case, Heinz Draw Ketchup:** Rethink asked people in 18 countries to "Draw Ketchup"; most drew a Heinz bottle (Rethink entry text, One Show and D&AD, T1). Gold Lion, Print and Publishing, Cannes 2022 (Strategy magazine 2022-06-20, T2). Agency-reported results: 97% of participants drew Heinz, sales +7.26% vs prior year in Canada, 127x publicity vs investment (Strategy Agency of the Year entry, T1, **claim**).
- **Counter-proof, Tropicana 2009:** see Section 8. Removing the orange-with-straw DBA cost an estimated 20% of unit sales in under two months.

### Design tokens
- Tokens are named, platform-neutral design decisions (color.brand.primary, space.4, font.display) stored as data and compiled into code for web, iOS, Android.
- The W3C Design Tokens Community Group published the **first stable Design Tokens Specification (2025.10) on 2025-10-28**, with theming and multi-brand support, Display P3 and OKLCH color, and aliasing (DTCG announcement, T1). It is a Community Group report, "not a W3C Standard" (W3C report page, T1). Supporting tools named include Style Dictionary, Tokens Studio, Terrazzo, Figma, Penpot, Sketch, Framer (DTCG, T1).
- **Agent behavior:** Every Bright Matter identity deliverable for a web client should ship a token file, not just a PDF. It is the bridge from guidelines to the website.

### Motion identity
- Define: easing curves, duration scale, how the logo enters and exits, transition grammar, kinetic type rules, what never moves.
- Example: Porto Rocha's PAC NYC square mark "expands to reveal all the possibilities within," and Brand New praised the "box rotation" motion (Porto Rocha site, T1; Brand New quote via Porto Rocha, T1).
- **Principle:** Motion is now the default first encounter (social, app open, pre-roll). Design the mark to move from the first sketch.

### Sonic branding
- Mnemonics people recognize: Intel bong, Netflix "ta-dum," McDonald's "ba da ba ba ba." Mastercard launched a full sonic brand in 2019 (Canon).
- **Sound of Honda / Ayrton Senna 1989 (Dentsu Tokyo, 2014):** rebuilt Senna's 1989 Suzuka qualifying lap as light and engine sound from real telemetry. Won the Titanium and Integrated Grand Prix and 15 Lions in 8 categories at Cannes 2014, plus a D&AD Black Pencil (Honda press release 2014-06-23, T1; Dentsu, T1).
- **Principle:** Sound is a DBA. A short, ownable audio cue is cheap and works in feeds where 30-second spots die.

### Brand guidelines anatomy
1. Platform summary (one page: purpose, positioning, personality)
2. Logo: construction, clear space, minimum size, lockups, misuse
3. Color: primary, secondary, ratios, accessibility pairs, token names
4. Typography: families, hierarchy, sizes, line length, licensing
5. Imagery: photography direction (light, casting, crop), illustration, iconography
6. Layout: grids, margins, compositional devices
7. Motion and sound
8. Voice and tone: do and don't rewrites
9. Applications: social templates, ads, signage, packaging, web components
10. Governance: who approves, where files live, how to request exceptions

Désirée Bambynek argues static guidelines are being replaced by "living brand systems" with "clear protocols for brand expression" rather than pre-approving every decision (Undercurrent newsletter, 2025-01-30, T3). Build guidelines that teach judgment, not just rules.

**Cross-case principles**
1. **Find the owned asset before drawing anything new** (Mastercard circles, Twitch purple, Amazon smile).
2. **One sentence of strategy should be visible in the mark** (Airbnb "belonging," Dropbox "syncing teams," PAC NYC "The World's Stage").
3. **Systems beat logos.** Every case above won on type, color, layout, and motion rules, not the mark alone.
4. **Local roots travel** (MASP's Brazilian modernism, Brooklyn's flag colors).
5. **Launch backlash is normal.** Airbnb was mocked in 2014 and is now invisible-familiar (Creative Review, T2). Judge at 12 months, not 12 hours.

## 7. Rebrand failures and contested cases

### Gap, 2010 (T2)
- **What happened:** A new logo (Helvetica "Gap" with a small blue square) appeared on gap.com around Oct 6, 2010. Designed by Gap with Laird & Partners. Gap reverted to the 20-year-old blue box on Oct 11-12 (NYT Media Decoder 2010-10-12, T2; BBC 2010-10-12, T2).
- **Backlash scale:** 2,000+ critical Facebook comments, a protest Twitter account with nearly 5,000 followers, about 14,000 parody logos on a "make your own" site (The Guardian 2010-10-12, T2).
- **What went wrong:**
  - Discarded the single most recognizable asset (the blue box with serif caps) for a generic default typeface.
  - No story connecting the change to anything customers felt.
  - Then crowdsourced the fix mid-crisis, which signaled no conviction. Gap's president admitted "we did not go about this in the right way" (The Guardian, T2).
- **Lesson:** Change must be earned by a strategy the public can feel. Never replace a DBA with a default.

### Tropicana, 2009 (T2)
- **What happened:** PepsiCo's Tropicana Pure Premium swapped the orange-with-a-straw for a glass of juice and a cap shaped like half an orange, designed by Arnell Group. Reverted Feb 23, 2009, after less than two months (Bloomberg 2009-04-02 quoting Ad Age, T2).
- **Numbers:** Unit sales fell 20% and dollar sales 19% (about $33 million) between Jan 1 and Feb 22, per Information Resources Inc. (Ad Age via Bloomberg, T2). Competitors Minute Maid, Florida's Natural, and Tree Ripe gained (same source). An academic study estimated the loss at about $27 million (Lee, Gao, Brown 2010, via ResearchGate, T3). Adweek reported the redesign and campaign at $35 million (Adweek AgencySpy 2009-02-23, T2, reported not confirmed).
- **What went wrong:** On a shelf, shoppers navigate by shape and color in seconds. The new pack looked like private label. Changes were reportedly not tested with consumers (Ritson 2009, cited in the academic literature, T3).
- **Lesson:** Packaging is wayfinding. Test recognition at shelf speed before launch.

### Jaguar "Copy Nothing," November 2024 (T2, debate ongoing)
- **What happened:** Jaguar Land Rover launched a rebrand in November 2024 with a teaser film of brightly dressed models and no cars, lines like "Delete Ordinary," "Create Exuberant," and "Copy Nothing," a new geometric logo replacing the "growler" cat, then the Type 00 concept at Miami Art Week in December 2024 (Consultancy.uk 2025-05-23, T2; The Guardian 2024-12-03, T2). Creative account held by Accenture Song with in-house agency Spark44 (Telegraph via archive.ph 2025-08-04, T2).
- **Reaction:** Mocked by Elon Musk ("Do you sell cars?") and Nigel Farage; Jaguar's MD Rawdon Glover condemned "vile hatred and intolerance" toward the cast (DesignRush 2025-05-12, T2; The Australia Today 2025-05-13, T3).
- **Context numbers:** Jaguar sales were already falling: 161,601 (2019), 61,661 (2022), 33,320 (2024) (The Australia Today citing The Telegraph, T3). JLR posted a £200 million profit-before-tax boost in the launch quarter (Consultancy.uk, T2). Bizcommunity claims the rebrand caused the decline (T3); that is weak, because the decline predates the campaign and Jaguar had stopped selling most models as a "fire break" (Car Dealer Magazine 2026-04-07, T2).
- **Aftermath:** JLR put its creative account under review in May 2025 while saying it was not because of the backlash (Business Now Malta 2025-05-15, T2). Chief Creative Officer Gerry McGovern has since left (The Independent 2025-12-19, T2; Car Dealer Magazine, T2). Dealer count cut from about 80 to under 20 in the UK (Car Dealer Magazine, T2). The production car (reported as **Type 01**) was due to be revealed in early October 2026 (Drive.com.au 2026-09-30, T2). **Verdict is pending as of 2026-10-03.**
- **The debate, both sides:**
  - *For:* Attention was the goal and they got it. "No amount of money" could buy that awareness (Evans, quoted in Car Dealer Magazine, T2). Jaguar intended to leave up to 90% of its old owner base behind to move to a roughly £120,000 price point (Car Dealer Magazine, T2).
  - *Against:* The campaign showed no product, discarded a heritage symbol that "could have been modernised with ease," and "so much goodwill was destroyed" (Motor Trade News 2026-03-20, T2). Launch timing just after the US election amplified culture-war readings (Car Dealer Magazine, T2).
- **Lesson for the agent:**
  - Separate three questions: Was the **strategy** right (go upmarket, go electric)? Was the **identity** right (drop the leaper)? Was the **launch sequence** right (abstract teaser before product)? Most critiques blur them.
  - Teasing a repositioning without the product leaves a vacuum the internet fills with its own story.
  - Deliberately alienating a base is a legitimate strategy only if the new audience sees something to want.

### Pattern across failures
1. Removed a distinctive asset without a replacement anyone recognized.
2. No visible "why" connecting change to customer benefit.
3. Treated launch as a reveal, not a sequence (Gap and Tropicana had no story; Jaguar had story without product).
4. Under-tested at the speed people actually encounter the brand (shelf, feed).

## 8. Big idea vs execution; campaign platform vs campaign

- **Big idea:** the single, transferable thought that can be expressed many ways. Test: can three different creatives make three different executions from it?
- **Execution:** one expression (a film, a poster, a TikTok, a stunt).
- **Campaign platform:** a big idea built to last years and absorb many campaigns. Usually carries a line. Examples (Canon): "Just Do It" (Nike, 1988 onward), "Priceless" (Mastercard, from 1997), "Think Different" (Apple, 1997), Dove "Real Beauty" (from 2004).
- **Campaign:** a time-bound burst under a platform with its own idea and executions (Dove "Real Beauty Sketches" 2013 under the Real Beauty platform).

**Test ladder the agent should apply**
1. Is there a problem worth solving? (Pollard's Problem)
2. Is there an insight that reframes it? (unspoken human truth)
3. Is the idea expressible in one sentence without the logo?
4. Could it run for three years? If yes, it may be a platform.
5. Does the execution contain a **craft choice** that makes the idea unmissable (see Section 13)?

## 9. Integrated campaign anatomy

| Layer | Role | Typical assets |
|---|---|---|
| **Platform / idea** | The one thought | Line, manifesto, key visual |
| **Hero** | Biggest expression, sets the tone | Film, stunt, OOH takeover, launch event |
| **Always-on social** | Native, frequent, reactive | Short video, memes, creator content, community replies |
| **Creator and partner** | Borrowed voices and audiences | Creator collabs, talent, brand collabs |
| **Earned / PR** | Something worth reporting | The stunt, the data, the controversy |
| **Owned** | Where interest lands | Site, landing page, CRM, app |
| **Retail / conversion** | Where money changes hands | Packaging, POS, promo, paid performance |
| **Measurement** | Proof | Brand lift, sales, search, sentiment |

**Worked example, The Ordinary "The Periodic Fable" (Uncommon Creative Studio London, launched October 2025):**
- Idea: a periodic table with "zero science," replacing elements with **49 skincare marketing buzzwords** like "Magic," "Medical Grade," "Fat Freezing," built with scientists (One Club entry, T1; D&AD, T1).
- Hero: a dystopian classroom film directed by Olivia De Camps where hypnotized students perform viral skincare rituals (D&AD Behind the Work 2026-09-16, T1; The Drum 2025-10-15, T2).
- OOH: single "elements" in isolation, plus full-table murals in global cities (One Club, T1).
- Owned: the table lives permanently on theordinary.com as a clickable, open-source tool (One Club, T1).
- Creator and PR: influencer kits with printed posters and bound index books (One Club, T1).
- Craft choice: "Brutal typography. Simple layouts. Zero ornamentation. Each word treated like a chemical symbol" (One Club entry, T1). Restraint in a loud category.
- Results reported by the entrant: 11.8x website traffic, +10.4% purchase intent among US women, 80+ press pieces (One Club, T1, **claim**). Won a D&AD Black Pencil (D&AD, T1).
- **Transferable principle:** Borrow a format everyone already knows how to read (the periodic table) and fill it with your argument. The format does the explaining.

**Worked example, Coinbase "Your Way Out" (Isle of Any with Coinbase Creative Studio, Oscars, March 2026):**
- Idea: "Life's a game, sometimes it feels like someone else has the controller" (Laurie Howell, shots 2026-03-12, T2).
- Craft: PS2-era game aesthetics recreated **in camera** with flattened 2D costumes, low-res set textures, actors choreographed as NPCs, a 75m set morphing from game to reality; directed by Oscar Hudson through MJZ (shots, T2; It's Nice That 2026-03-26, T2).
- Why in camera: "in order for you to identify with this character, to feel the emotion, it's kind of essential for it to be shot for real" (Toby Treyer-Evans, D&AD 2026-09-15, T1). Won a D&AD Black Pencil (D&AD, T1).
- Platform continuity: Coinbase's line "It's time to update the system," which also drove Isle of Any's 2025 blue-screen-of-death ASCII campaign for the NBA Playoffs (It's Nice That 2025-05-07, T2).
- **Transferable principle:** One platform, radically different executions per media moment (Super Bowl singalong, Oscars cinema, NBA glitch). Consistency lives in the idea, not the look.

## 10. The brief

### What a creative brief is for
A brief is "a brave attempt to capture only the most important words" (Pollard, markpollard.net, T1). Its job is to make the creative team's first idea better than it would have been.

### Single-minded proposition (SMP)
- One thing to say. Not three. If the client insists on three, rank them and brief the first.
- Test: can you say it in one breath? Does it imply a tension?
- Bad: "Fresh, local, and affordable." Good: "The only grocery where the farmer's name is on the receipt."

### Get / To / By
- Format: **Get** [audience, defined by a mindset or behavior] **To** [do or believe one specific thing] **By** [the persuasive idea or message].
- Pollard teaches it alongside the 4Cs and Four Points and says no framework is "right" (Sweathead podcast episode "The 4Cs, Get/To/By, and The Four Points," 2021-10-01, T1; Sweathead lesson 21 "Get/To/By - Iceland," T1). Original author of Get/To/By: **unverified**.
- Example: Get *time-poor parents who think healthy dinners take an hour* To *try a weeknight meal kit* By *showing that the fastest meal in the house is the one already prepped.*

### One-page brief template (for the agent to fill)
1. **Background** (two sentences, the business situation)
2. **Problem** (the obstacle to buying or doing)
3. **Audience** (a person and a mindset, not a demographic)
4. **Insight** ("But here's the thing about...")
5. **Advantage** (what is true and motivating about this brand)
6. **Proposition / strategy** ("Show that X is Y")
7. **Get / To / By**
8. **Reasons to believe**
9. **Mandatories** (logo, legal, offer, sizes)
10. **Deliverables and channels**
11. **Success measure**
12. **Tone** (two adjectives and one "not")

## 11. Insight writing: what an insight is and is not

**Is:**
- "An unspoken human truth that sheds new light on the problem" (Pollard, JustCreative, T2/T3).
- An insight "updates how you see the world... gives you new words for something you knew existed but with magnified meaning" (Pollard, markpollard.net, T1).
- Usually a tension: people want X but do Y.

**Is not:**
- A statistic ("60% of body wash is bought by women" is a **fact**; the insight W+K built on it is that the purchase is a negotiation between couples, so speak to both at once) (W+K case page, T1; D&AD case, T1).
- An observation everyone already says out loud.
- A product benefit dressed as a human truth.

**Examples from verified work**
| Campaign | Fact | Insight |
|---|---|---|
| Dove Real Beauty Sketches | Women describe their own features more negatively than strangers do | "You are more beautiful than you think" (Ad Age 2013-06-22, T2) |
| The Ordinary Periodic Fable | Beauty claims often have no scientific or regulatory basis | "Beauty marketing works because it sounds scientific, even when it isn't" (One Club, T1) |
| Carling Black Label #NoExcuse | Partner violence spikes after football matches | Men will listen to the stadium anthem when they won't listen to a lecture (Ogilvy Cape Town; Bizcommunity 2017 and pressportal 2018, T2/T3) |
| Heinz Draw Ketchup | Heinz affinity scores were declining | People cannot picture ketchup without picturing Heinz (Rethink entry, T1) |

**Insight quality checklist**
- Would the audience nod and say "that's so true, I never said it"?
- Does it point toward an idea, without being the idea?
- Can you support it with evidence (reviews, interviews, behavior data)?

## 12. Team model and roles

### The Bernbach creative team (Canon)
- Bill Bernbach co-founded Doyle Dane Bernbach (DDB) in 1949 and is credited with institutionalizing the **copywriter plus art director pair** working together from the start, instead of copy being written first and handed to a layout artist. Canon.
- Result: ideas where words and pictures are inseparable (VW "Think Small," see Section 13).
- Jason Bagley's ASAP school still teaches the pairing explicitly with separate tracks, "Creative Megamachine" and "Art Director Megamachine" (the latter with FCB global CCO Danilo Boer and creative director Lawrence Melilli) (MM+M 2025-05-27, T2).

### Role definitions (practitioner synthesis)
| Role | Owns | Question they ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Creative director (CD)** | The idea and the standard across all work | "Is this the right idea and is it good enough?" |
| **Associate / group CD** | A team or account's output | "Is the team solving the brief?" |
| **Art director (AD)** | The visual concept and how the idea looks and feels | "What do we see, and why will it stop someone?" |
| **Copywriter** | The verbal concept, lines, scripts, voice | "What do we say, and how?" |
| **Designer** | Craft execution and systems | "How do we make this precise, consistent, scalable?" |
| **Strategist / planner** | Problem, audience, insight, brief | "Are we solving the right problem?" |
| **Producer** | Making it real on budget | "Can we make it, and with whom?" |
| **Brand designer / identity lead** | The identity system | "Will this hold up in 500 applications?" |

**Art direction vs design, in one line:** the art director decides what the picture should be and why; the designer decides exactly how it is built. In small teams one person does both, but the agent should name which hat it is wearing.

## 13. Famous campaigns: idea, craft choice, transferable principle

### Era 1: the creative revolution and print canon
**Volkswagen "Think Small" (DDB, 1959)** (Canon)
- Idea: celebrate the car's smallness instead of hiding it.
- Craft: a tiny Beetle in a sea of white space, headline under the image, honest and self-deprecating copy. Art director Helmut Krone, copywriter Julian Koenig. Named the top ad of the 20th century by Ad Age (1999).
- Principle: **White space is a statement.** Make the weakness the point.

**Absolut (TBWA, from 1980)** (Canon)
- Idea: the bottle silhouette is the brand; every ad is "Absolut [Something]."
- Craft: one rigid format (bottle shape plus two-word headline) with endless artist and city variations, including Warhol in 1985.
- Principle: **A fixed format plus infinite variation builds a DBA faster than any logo.**

### Era 2: brand as belief
**Apple "1984" (Chiat/Day, Ridley Scott)** (Canon)
- Idea: Macintosh as the escape from conformity.
- Craft: a cinematic dystopia aired nationally once, during the Super Bowl in January 1984. The product is never shown.
- Principle: **Sell the worldview; the product can wait.** (Compare Jaguar 2024: this only works if the product arrives fast and delivers.)

**Apple "Think Different" (TBWA\Chiat\Day, 1997)** (Canon)
- Idea: Apple belongs with the "crazy ones" who changed the world.
- Craft: black-and-white portraits of icons, small logo, grammatically "wrong" line.
- Principle: **Borrowed meaning.** Align with people your audience already admires.

**Nike "Just Do It" (Wieden+Kennedy, 1988)** (Canon)
- Idea: sport as personal will, open to everyone.
- Craft: three words that fit any athlete, any sport, any era; W+K founder Dan Wieden wrote the line.
- Principle: **A platform line should be short, imperative, and endlessly re-castable.**

### Era 3: digital and social breakthroughs
**Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" (W+K Portland, February 2010)**
- Idea: talk to women and men at once about a product women mostly buy (W+K, T1).
- Craft: a single-take, absurdist one-shot with Isaiah Mustafa, then the **Response campaign**: 186 personal videos filmed in about two and a half days answering fans on Twitter, Facebook, Reddit (W+K, T1; D&AD, T1).
- Recognition: Cannes Film Grand Prix 2010 (Ad Age 2010-06-26, T2).
- Reported results: Red Zone body wash sales +60% by May 2010, doubled by July (W+K, T1, **claim**); D&AD's case says +125% year on year by end of July (D&AD, T1, **claim**). Note the two agency-side figures differ.
- Principle: **Make the hero film, then let the character live in real time.**

**Dove "Real Beauty Sketches" (Ogilvy Brazil, April 2013)**
- Idea: women underestimate their own beauty.
- Craft: an FBI-trained forensic artist, Gil Zamora, draws each woman twice from behind a curtain: once from her description, once from a stranger's (Wikipedia, T3; Ad Age, T2).
- Recognition: Titanium Grand Prix, Cannes 2013 (Adweek 2013-06-22, T2). 163 million global views by June 2013 (Wikipedia citing Google, T3).
- Criticism worth knowing: not a valid experiment; only some participants shown; cast skewed young, thin, mostly white (Wikipedia citing Bloomberg and Forbes, T3).
- Principle: **A visible, repeatable experiment makes an insight undeniable.** Also: proof devices invite scrutiny, so cast and edit honestly.

**Spotify Wrapped (in-house, from 2016)**
- Idea: your data, given back as a story about you, designed to be shared.
- History: "Year in Music" in 2015 drew 5 million unique users; rebranded "Wrapped" in 2016; built into the app in 2019 (Spotify Newsroom 2024-12-04, T1; Wikipedia, T3). A former Spotify staffer argues the work dates to 2011-2014 microsites (Differ blog 2024-12-16, T3).
- Craft: vertical story cards, bold color blocks and type, personal superlatives, a share button at every step.
- Principle: **Make the customer the hero and the brand the medium.** Design for the screenshot.

### Era 4: product truth, crisis, and anti-advertising
**KFC "FCK" (Mother London, February 2018)**
- Idea: a chicken shortage closed most UK stores; apologize like a human.
- Craft: the bucket with letters rearranged to "FCK," full-page print in UK newspapers, a plain-language apology underneath (Campaign 2018-06-28, T2).
- Recognition: three Golds and a Silver at Cannes 2018, including Gold in Print and Publishing (Campaign, T2; QSR Media UK, T2).
- Principle: **In a crisis, the brand's voice must survive.** Use your own asset (the bucket) to say sorry.

**Burger King "Moldy Whopper" (INGO Stockholm, David Miami, Publicis, February 2020)**
- Idea: prove "no artificial preservatives" by showing the Whopper rotting over 34 days.
- Craft: beautiful food-photography lighting applied to decay; time-lapse film; day-numbered posters (Day 28, 32, 35).
- Recognition: three Outdoor Grand Prix at Cannes 2021 for individual posters (Eastwind, T3); Epica Grand Prix in Film, Print, and PR (SHOOT 2020-12-19, T2); Gerety Grand Prix (Gerety Awards, T1). Burger King CMO Fernando Machado waited until the recipe change was real (D&AD, T1).
- Principle: **Show the uncomfortable proof, shot with total craft.** The beauty of the photography makes the ugliness persuasive.

**Heinz "Draw Ketchup" (Rethink, Toronto, 2021)**
- See Section 5. Craft: user drawings, unretouched, placed on premium OOH and limited-edition labels; best drawings auctioned (Strategy, T1).
- Principle: **Let the audience draw your DBA.** If they can, you own it.

**Liquid Death (founded by Mike Cessario, sales from January 2019)**
- Idea: water sold with heavy-metal and punk codes. "Murder your thirst."
- Craft: tallboy beer-style can, skull branding, comedic content. Cessario was an agency graphic designer; the founders trademarked the name two years before shipping and launched a fake-real teaser video first (Fortune 2024-03-14, T2; TechCrunch 2019-01-24, T2).
- Numbers: $1.4 billion valuation after a $67 million round, March 2024 (Fortune, T2); $263 million retail sales in 2023 (Wikipedia, T3).
- Eugene Healey's read: Liquid Death took "the semiotic codes of like rock" and put them into water, creating productive dissonance (Healey on That Business of Meaning, 2025-01-06, T3).
- Principle: **Semiotic dissonance.** Borrow the codes of a category your audience loves and apply them to one that bores them.

**Duolingo social (in-house, from 2020)**
- Idea: Duo the owl as an unhinged, slightly menacing character who lives on social.
- Craft: speed and reactivity. Grew from 50,000 to more than 16 million TikTok followers in a little over four years; all social is in-house because "Turnaround time is a week" (Zaria Parvez, The Drum 2025-02-25, T2).
- Peak: "Duo is dead" (February 2025), which began as a routine app-icon change and went from concept to execution in six days (The Drum, T2). Mentions spiked about 25,560% on Feb 11, 2025 (Meltwater 2025-02-20, T1 vendor data). Android daily downloads +38% the day after, per Similarweb (TechCrunch 2025-02-18, T2).
- Principle: **A character is the most flexible DBA for social.** Give it a consistent personality and let it react.

### Non-US work
**Japan: "Sound of Honda / Ayrton Senna 1989" (Dentsu, 2013-14)**
- Idea: Honda's data heritage made emotional.
- Craft: Suzuka circuit lined with speakers and LEDs, replaying Senna's lap from telemetry in the dark; sound reproduced from the real McLaren Honda MP4/5 (Honda, T1).
- Recognition: Cannes Titanium and Integrated Grand Prix 2014, 15 Lions; D&AD Black Pencil (Honda, T1).
- Principle: **Data becomes art when you choose the right sense.** Here, sound and light instead of charts.

**Brazil: "Immortal Fans" (Ogilvy Brazil for Sport Club do Recife, 2013)**
- Idea: the first organ-donor card for a football club, so a fan's passion lives on in others.
- Craft: real transplant-waitlist patients made the appeal directly to fans; it addressed Brazil's main barrier, family authorization (D&AD, T1; Adweek, T2).
- Recognition: Promo and Activation Grand Prix, Cannes 2013 (Ad Age 2013-06-17, T2; Adweek, T2).
- Principle: **Plug the cause into an existing passion.** Do not create a new emotion; redirect one.

**India: "Not Just a Cadbury Ad" and "Shah Rukh Khan-My-Ad" (Ogilvy Mumbai for Mondelez Cadbury Celebrations, 2020-21)**
- Idea: during the pandemic, Cadbury gave its ad time to local shops.
- Craft: machine learning generated hyper-local versions of a Shah Rukh Khan ad where he names nearby small businesses, depending on the viewer's location (Ogilvy, T1).
- Recognition: Grand Prix, Creative Effectiveness, Cannes 2023; Ogilvy India says it helped "over 200,000 small stores" (e4m 2023-06-23, T2; quote is **claim**).
- Principle: **Generosity at scale.** Personalization is strongest when the brand gives the spotlight away.

**South Africa: Carling Black Label "#NoExcuse" (Ogilvy Cape Town, 2017-18)**
- Idea: confront gender-based violence among the brand's own drinkers.
- Craft: before the Soweto Derby (Kaizer Chiefs vs Orlando Pirates, March 3, 2018), a women's choir sang the football anthem "Asambe Nono" with rewritten lyrics in front of about 85,000 fans (Modern Marketing 2018-03-01, T2).
- Recognition: Grand Prix (Radio and Audio), Cannes 2018 (pressportal.co.za 2018-07-03, T3; earlier campaign phase in Bizcommunity 2017-11-30, T2).
- Principle: **Hijack the ritual where the audience already pays attention.** A sung anthem beats a printed statistic.

**UK: The Ordinary "The Periodic Fable" (Uncommon, 2025)** and **US/UK: Coinbase "Your Way Out" (Isle of Any, 2026):** see Section 9.

### Cross-campaign craft patterns
1. **One device, held hard** (Think Small's white space, Absolut's bottle, FCK's bucket, Periodic Fable's table).
2. **Proof made visible** (Moldy Whopper's mold, Sketches' two drawings, Draw Ketchup's crayon bottles).
3. **Borrowed format** (periodic table, PS2 game, football anthem, forensic sketch).
4. **Restraint in a loud category** (The Ordinary's zero ornamentation).
5. **Character over campaign** for social (Duo, Old Spice Guy).

## 14. Presenting and selling the work

### The sequence that sells (adapted from Pollard's Four Points structure, T2/T3)
1. **Problem** (one slide, sharp)
2. **Evidence** (who you talked to, what they said)
3. **Insight** (the "but here's the thing")
4. **Advantage**
5. **Strategy / idea in one line**
6. **The work**, revealed in context
7. **How it scales** (platform map: hero, social, OOH, owned)
8. **What happens next** (production path, timing)

### Show it in situ
- Never present a logo on white alone. Show it on the storefront, app icon, uniform, social avatar, receipt, van.
- Never present a social post as a flat square. Show it in a phone frame in a real feed with the posts around it.
- Tools: Maneken for drag-and-drop mockups with video support (maneken.app, T1); see file 02 for the full tool list.

### Pre-production artifacts and when to use them
| Artifact | What it is | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Mood board** | Collected references for feeling, palette, light, casting | Aligning on tone before any original work; label every reference with what to take from it |
| **Style frames** | A few finished-quality stills of key moments | Motion or film work, to lock the look |
| **Storyboard** | Sequential frames with action and dialogue | Any film, to agree on story and shots |
| **Animatic** | Storyboard timed to sound | Testing pace and story before a shoot |
| **Lookbook / treatment** | Director's or AD's visual argument | Film pitches, photo shoots |
| **Mockups in situ** | Work placed in real contexts | Identity, packaging, OOH, social |
| **Deck** | The argument | Always; the work must live inside a story |

Shot references: ShotDeck (founded by cinematographer Lawrence Sher, millions of tagged film stills, T1), shot.cafe, FILMGRAB, frameset.app. Moodboarding: Cosmos, Are.na, Adobe Firefly Boards (launched worldwide 2025-09-24, Adobe Blog, T1).

### Selling tips (practitioner synthesis)
- Present one recommendation with conviction, then alternatives. Three equal options invite a committee to merge them.
- Name the risk yourself before the client does, and say why it is worth it.
- Tie every visual choice to a platform word ("We used one color because the brand promise is simplicity").
- For rebrands, show what you **kept** before what you changed (Mastercard logic).

## 15. Taste and visual fluency as skills

Taste is trainable. It is the speed and accuracy with which you can say "this, not that, because."

**Working definitions**
- **Taste:** a consistent point of view about what is good, plus the ability to explain it.
- **Visual fluency:** recognizing references, eras, techniques, and their cultural meaning on sight (a Swiss grid, a 1970s NASA worm, a PS2 render, Dada collage).

**How practitioners describe it**
- Tommy Geoco spent a season following creative teams for 48 hours each for his docuseries *This Is Taste* and concluded taste was not mainly "the details... the branding decisions and interaction patterns." He says he "was wrong" about that (Geoco, "I was wrong about taste," Substack 2026-05-22, T1).
- Nathan Williams's *The Eye* (Artisan, 2018) profiles 90+ creative directors on how they "developed their eye," including the books they read and mentors who guided them (Hachette, T1; Creative Review 2018-10-25, T2).
- Szilveszter Makó works "from A to B... It is remarkable what you can produce within a restricted parameter" (Aperture 2026-06-18, T2). Constraint as a taste engine.

**Training routine for the agent and team**
1. **Reference diet:** save 10 references a day to a named board, each tagged with *why*.
2. **Name the technique:** for every reference, write the technique (e.g., "split-toning," "flat lay," "single-take," "chiaroscuro").
3. **Copy to learn:** recreate one reference a week exactly, then change one variable.
4. **Critique in pairs:** "What is the one decision that makes this work?"
5. **Cross-discipline:** cities (Talking Cities), interiors (Heidi Caillier), fashion photography (Makó), music criticism (Derrick Gee). See file 02.
6. **Kill your darlings:** for each concept, write the strongest argument against it.

## 16. Quick diagnostics the agent can run

**Platform check**
- [ ] Positioning names a competitive alternative
- [ ] Onliness statement completes with "only"
- [ ] Personality could be cast as a real actor
- [ ] Voice has do and don't rewrite examples

**Identity check**
- [ ] Owned assets inventoried before new design
- [ ] Logo tested at 16 px and on a van
- [ ] Motion behavior defined
- [ ] Token file exists for web clients
- [ ] Guidelines include governance

**Campaign check**
- [ ] One-sentence idea without the logo
- [ ] Insight passes "never said it out loud"
- [ ] A named craft choice makes the idea unmissable
- [ ] Executions planned per channel, not resized
- [ ] Proof of claim is visible, not asserted

**Rebrand risk check**
- [ ] What DBA are we removing, and what replaces its recognition?
- [ ] Have we tested at shelf or feed speed?
- [ ] Does the launch sequence show the "why" and the product?
- [ ] Do we have a plan for the first 72 hours of backlash?

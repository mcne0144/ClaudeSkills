# Visual Vocabulary: the art director's dictionary

Purpose: give the agent (and Shannon) precise words for what a visual does, so feedback, briefs, prompts and vendor direction stop sounding like "make it pop." Organized by domain. Each term: a plain definition, then **Use** (how to deploy it in direction or critique). Movement-level history lives in `03-art-history-atlas.md` and `04-design-history-and-typography.md`; film and web craft in `09-video-web-and-motion-craft.md`.

Jason Swet's rule applies throughout: three levels of language. **Feeling** (for clients), **metaphor** (for teams), **ingredient** (for vendors). This file is the ingredient level.

---

## 1. Elements of visual form

- **Line**: a mark with length and direction. Contour line (outer edge), gesture line (energy of a pose), implied line (eyelines, pointing, aligned edges the eye connects). **Use**: direct the gaze with implied lines toward the logo or CTA.
- **Shape**: a closed 2D area. Geometric (circle, square, triangle; read as engineered, stable) vs organic (biomorphic, natural, friendly). **Use**: logo personality; rounded = approachable, sharp angles = speed or aggression.
- **Form**: 3D volume, real or implied by shading.
- **Space**: positive space (the subject) and negative space (around it). **Use**: negative space signals confidence and premium; crowding signals urgency or discount.
- **Value**: lightness to darkness, independent of hue. The single most important property for legibility and hierarchy. **Use**: squint test; if the design falls apart in grayscale, it falls apart.
- **Texture**: actual (tactile, in print or material) or implied (visual surface). **Use**: grain, paper, fabric add warmth and "handmade" credibility in an AI-smooth feed.
- **Color**: see section 4.
- **Mass / visual weight**: how much attention an element pulls. Larger, darker, more saturated, more textured, more isolated, or a human face = heavier.

## 2. Principles of design

- **Hierarchy**: the order in which the eye reads. Built with scale, value, color, position, isolation, typography. Every layout needs exactly one first read.
- **Contrast**: difference that creates emphasis. Of value, color, scale, texture, direction, density, and (per Jason Swet) of subjects and contexts ("high art shoved into the mundane").
- **Balance**: symmetrical (formal, stable, institutional), asymmetrical (dynamic, modern, balanced by visual weight), radial (emanating from a center).
- **Emphasis / focal point**: where attention lands first. **Isolation effect** (von Restorff): the one thing different from its neighbors is remembered.
- **Rhythm**: repetition with variation creating tempo. Regular, alternating, progressive, flowing.
- **Repetition / pattern**: repeated elements build a system and recognition. Repetition is what turns a motif into a brand (see Bright Matter's punched dot).
- **Proportion and scale**: size relationships. Scale shift (making a familiar thing huge or tiny) is one of the cheapest ways to make an ad strange enough to notice. **Scale disparity** (Jason Swet, 2026) is the named technique: dramatically different sizes put together in one frame.
- **Unity / harmony**: everything feels like one family. **Variety** keeps unity from becoming monotony.
- **Movement / directional cues**: how the eye travels. Diagonals create energy; horizontals calm; verticals assert.
- **Alignment**: elements share edges or axes. Invisible alignment is what makes "amateur" look "designed."
- **Proximity**: related things sit together.
- **White space** (negative space as an active element). Also called breathing room.
- **Tension**: elements placed near an edge or almost touching. Uncomfortable, attention-holding.
- **Restraint**: choosing not to add. Often the strongest art direction decision in a client room.

## 3. Composition and framing

- **Rule of thirds**: placing subjects on a 3x3 grid's intersections. A convention, useful as a starting point, not a law.
- **Golden ratio / golden spiral** (1:1.618): widely claimed in design and nature; evidence that viewers prefer it is weak and contested. Use as a proportion tool, never as a selling argument.
- **Center framing / symmetrical framing**: frontal, confrontational, iconic (Wes Anderson, Kubrick one-point perspective).
- **Leading lines**: roads, rails, arms, edges pointing to the subject.
- **Frame within a frame**: doorways, windows, mirrors, screens. Adds depth and voyeurism. Jason Swet teaches it as a named art direction technique: use an opening or boundary inside the image to frame the subject.
- **Foreground interest / layering**: something close to the lens creates depth. Jason's "think 2D" tip: a foreground element and an empty room still reads as a designed set.
- **Fill the frame / tight crop**: intimacy, intensity, detail (Jason's favorite: "awkwardly close-up photography").
- **Dutch angle (canted)**: tilted horizon, unease.
- **High angle / low angle / bird's eye / worm's eye**: power relationships. Low angle makes the subject dominant.
- **Headroom, lead room (nose room)**: space above the head; space in the direction a subject faces or moves.
- **Z-pattern and F-pattern**: scanning paths for simple and text-heavy layouts respectively (eye-tracking origin; see web craft file for evidence status).
- **Gutenberg diagram**: primary optical area top-left, terminal area bottom-right in left-to-right reading cultures. Flip for right-to-left (Arabic, Hebrew) markets.
- **Figure/ground**: the relationship between subject and background. **Figure/ground reversal** makes the negative space the subject (FedEx arrow, Rubin's vase, WWF panda). Jason's fix for "too obvious."
- **Juxtaposition**: placing two things side by side to create a third meaning.
- **Mise-en-scène**: everything placed in front of the camera: set, props, costume, actors, light, blocking.
- **Blocking**: where subjects stand and move in the frame.
- **Breathing the frame**: deliberately leaving space for type, logos, platform UI (safe zones in 9:16).

## 4. Color

### Properties
- **Hue**: the color family (red, blue).
- **Saturation / chroma**: intensity or purity. Low = muted, dusty, sophisticated; high = loud, youthful, urgent.
- **Value / lightness / brightness**: how light or dark.
- **Tint** (hue + white), **shade** (+ black), **tone** (+ gray).
- **Temperature**: warm (reds, oranges, yellows, advance toward viewer) vs cool (blues, greens, recede).
- **Color models**: RGB (screens, additive), CMYK (process print, subtractive), spot colors (Pantone Matching System, exact ink), HSL/HSB (designer-friendly), Lab and **OKLCH** (perceptually uniform; modern CSS uses OKLCH for consistent palettes).
- **Gamut**: the range a device or process can reproduce. Neon RGB oranges often fall outside CMYK; plan spot colors or expect dulling in print.

### Harmonies
- Monochromatic, analogous (neighbors on the wheel), complementary (opposites, maximum vibrance), split-complementary, triadic, tetradic/square.
- **60-30-10**: dominant, secondary, accent proportion. A starting convention. Bright Matter's palette works this way: one accent (orange) owns every CTA.

### Interaction and contrast (Itten and Albers)
- **Johannes Itten's seven contrasts** (Bauhaus): contrast of hue, light-dark, cold-warm, complementary, simultaneous, saturation, extension (proportion).
- **Simultaneous contrast** (Josef Albers, *Interaction of Color*): the same color looks different depending on its neighbors. **Use**: always judge brand colors in context, never on a white artboard.
- **Vibrating boundaries**: two saturated complements of similar value at an edge seem to buzz. Op Art used it; legibility suffers.
- **Color blocking**: large flat fields of solid color.
- **Duotone / tritone**: an image mapped to two or three inks. Spotify's 2015 identity era made duotone a brand device.
- **Color grading** (film): teal and orange, bleach bypass, crushed blacks, lifted blacks (faded film), split toning. See the video craft file.
- **Palette roles**: ground, ink, accent, support, signal (success, warning, error).

### Color meaning
- Color associations are **learned and cultural**, not universal. White is mourning in parts of East and South Asia and bridal in the West; red is luck and celebration in China, danger or sale in the US; green carries religious meaning in many Muslim-majority countries. Research on color psychology is mostly correlational and context-dependent (see `06-advertising-science-and-psychology.md` for evidence status). **Use**: argue color from category codes, brand distinctiveness, and contrast, not from "blue means trust."

## 5. Light

- **Key light** (main), **fill light** (softens shadows), **back / rim / hair light** (separates subject from background), **kicker**, **practicals** (visible lamps in frame), **motivated lighting** (light that appears to come from a real source).
- **Hard light** (small or distant source, crisp shadows, drama, fashion flash) vs **soft light** (large diffused source, gentle falloff, beauty, lifestyle).
- **High key** (bright, low contrast, optimistic, clean) vs **low key** (dark, high contrast, mysterious, premium).
- **Chiaroscuro**: strong light-dark modeling (Leonardo, Rembrandt). **Tenebrism**: extreme version with figures emerging from darkness (Caravaggio).
- **Portrait patterns**: Rembrandt (triangle of light on the shadowed cheek), butterfly / Paramount (shadow under the nose, glamour), loop, split (half the face dark), broad vs short lighting.
- **Color temperature** in Kelvin: candle about 1,900K, tungsten about 3,200K, daylight about 5,600K, overcast and shade cooler. Mixed temperatures create tension or realism.
- **Golden hour / blue hour / magic hour**.
- **Direct flash / on-camera flash**: hard, flat, paparazzi and party aesthetic (Juergen Teller, Terry Richardson era, revived by 2020s editorial and Gen Z digicam culture).
- **Gels**: colored filters on lights; neon cyberpunk looks.
- **Specular highlight**: the bright reflection on glossy surfaces. Product photography lives and dies by controlling speculars (strip lights on bottles, gradient reflections).
- **Volumetric light / god rays / haze**: visible beams through atmosphere.
- **Caustics**: patterns of light focused through water or glass (beverage, fragrance, pool aesthetics).
- **Silhouette and contre-jour**: shooting into the light.

## 6. Photography and image-making terms

- **Focal length**: wide (14 to 35mm; distortion, environment, energy), normal (around 50mm; human eye feel), telephoto (85mm and up; compression, flattering portraits, isolation).
- **Lens compression**: telephoto makes background appear closer and larger.
- **Depth of field**: shallow (blurry background, subject isolation, intimacy) vs deep (everything sharp, documentary, environmental).
- **Bokeh**: the quality of out-of-focus areas.
- **Aperture (f-stop), shutter speed, ISO**: the exposure triangle. Slow shutter = motion blur (dragging the shutter with flash freezes subject and streaks light).
- **Tilt-shift**: selective focus plane making real scenes look miniature.
- **Fisheye / lens distortion / prism and kaleidoscope filters** (Jason's Oakley "delusion" spec shoot).
- **Film stock references**: Kodak Portra (warm skin, pastel), Ektachrome (saturated slide), Tri-X (gritty B&W), CineStill 800T (tungsten-balanced, red halation around highlights), Polaroid / instant (soft, washed, borders), disposable camera (flash, grain, light leaks).
- **Grain, halation, light leaks, vignetting, chromatic aberration**: analog imperfections; signal authenticity, nostalgia, or "not AI."
- **Editorial vs commercial vs documentary vs lifestyle vs still life vs product (packshot) vs hero shot**.
- **Flat lay**: overhead arrangement of objects.
- **In situ mockup**: the work shown in its real environment (billboard in a street, package on a shelf). The most persuasive way to sell a concept (Jason: "mock up exactly what you have in your head").
- **Retouching**: frequency separation, dodge and burn, compositing, clean plate.
- **Stock vs commissioned vs UGC vs AI-generated**: provenance matters for rights, authenticity, and platform disclosure.

## 7. Typography (working vocabulary; history in file 04)

- **Typeface** (the design) vs **font** (a specific weight/style file).
- **Serif / sans serif / slab serif / script / display / monospace / blackletter**.
- **Grotesque, neo-grotesque, geometric, humanist** sans serifs.
- **Old style, transitional, didone (modern), slab, glyphic** serifs.
- **Weight** (hairline to black), **width** (condensed to extended), **optical size**.
- **Variable font**: one file with continuous axes (weight, width, slant, custom).
- **Kerning** (space between two specific letters), **tracking / letter-spacing** (uniform spacing across a word), **leading / line-height**.
- **x-height, cap height, ascender, descender, baseline, counter, aperture, bowl, stem, terminal, ligature, swash**.
- **Measure**: line length; roughly 45 to 75 characters per line is the classic readable range.
- **Rag**: the ragged edge of left-aligned text; good rag avoids awkward shapes. **Widows and orphans**: stranded single words or lines.
- **Hierarchy tools**: size, weight, case, color, spacing.
- **All caps tracking**: open up tracking on all-caps to keep it legible and premium.
- **Type as image / expressive typography / kinetic typography**: letters do the visual work.
- **Lockup**: a fixed arrangement of logo and wordmark or tagline.
- **Wordmark, lettermark/monogram, logotype**.
- **Type pairing**: contrast in structure with harmony in proportion (for example a high-contrast serif display with a quiet grotesque body).

## 8. Layout and grids

- **Grid**: columns, gutters, margins, rows, modules, baseline grid.
- **Manuscript grid** (single column, books), **column grid** (editorial, web), **modular grid** (rows and columns, Swiss style), **hierarchical grid** (custom, web dashboards), **bento grid** (modular cards of varying sizes, popular in 2020s product marketing).
- **Breaking the grid**: intentional violation for emphasis. Only works when the grid is otherwise felt.
- **Bleed**: imagery extending past the trim edge. **Full bleed**.
- **Safe zone / live area**: where critical content must sit (print trim, social UI overlays).
- **Above the fold**: visible without scrolling (print newspaper origin, web reuse).
- **Visual rhythm in decks**: alternating full-bleed image slides with type slides.
- **Density**: sparse (luxury, editorial) vs dense (retail, information, maximalism).

## 9. Print and graphic production techniques

- **Halftone**: dots simulating continuous tone; enlarged as a pop or retro device (Lichtenstein's Ben-Day dots).
- **Risograph**: stencil-duplicator printing with vivid soy inks, slight misregistration and grain; indie zine and studio aesthetic.
- **Screen printing / serigraphy**: flat, opaque layers (Warhol, gig posters).
- **Letterpress**: relief printing with impression into paper; artisanal premium.
- **Offset lithography**: the standard commercial print process.
- **Foil stamping, embossing, debossing, spot UV, die-cut, soft-touch laminate**: packaging and stationery finishing; signal premium through touch.
- **Misregistration**: layers slightly out of alignment; deliberate to look printed and human.
- **Overprint / multiply**: transparent inks mixing where they overlap.
- **Cyanotype**: blue sun-print photography; botanical and craft aesthetic.
- **Collage / photomontage / mixed media**: cut and assembled imagery (Hannah Höch, Dada, John Heartfield; 2020s social collage trend).
- **Xerox / photocopy aesthetic**: high contrast, degraded, punk.
- **Linocut / woodcut**: carved relief prints, bold and folk (ukiyo-e, German Expressionism).
- **Engraving / etching / stipple**: fine line shading; currency, heritage brands.
- **Visible facture**: process left visible (brushstrokes, pen pressure, cut edges). Signals human authorship.

## 10. Illustration styles

- Line art, flat vector, isometric, editorial conceptual illustration, gouache and painterly, watercolor, ink wash, charcoal, pastel, pixel art, low-poly, claymation look, paper cut, children's book, manga and anime (shonen, shojo, chibi), ligne claire (Hergé's clear line), rubber hose (1930s cartoons), mid-century modern (UPA, Mary Blair), psychedelic, botanical, scientific illustration, infographics, technical/exploded views, blind contour, doodle, Corporate Memphis (flat people with tiny heads and big limbs; widely parodied as generic tech).

## 11. 3D and CGI vocabulary

- **Modeling**: polygons, vertices, edges, faces; **topology** (how polygons flow; clean quads deform well), **retopology**, **subdivision surface**, **sculpting** (ZBrush-style organic detail), **procedural modeling** (rules generate geometry; Houdini, Geometry Nodes), **photogrammetry** (3D from many photos), **Gaussian splatting** (radiance-field scenes captured from video).
- **UV unwrapping**: flattening a model's surface so textures map onto it.
- **Materials and shading**: **PBR** (physically based rendering) channels: base color/albedo, roughness, metallic, normal map (fake surface detail), bump/height, displacement (real geometry change), ambient occlusion, emission, opacity. **Subsurface scattering** (light passing into skin, wax, jade, milk). **Fresnel** (surfaces get more reflective at glancing angles). **Anisotropy** (brushed metal streaks). **Iridescence / thin film** (soap bubbles, chrome Y2K).
- **Lighting**: HDRI environment maps, area lights, three-point, global illumination, ray tracing, path tracing.
- **Rendering**: real-time (Unreal Engine, Blender Eevee, game engines) vs offline (Blender Cycles, Octane, Redshift, Arnold, V-Ray). **Render passes / AOVs** for compositing.
- **Rigging** (skeleton and controls), **skinning**, **blend shapes / morph targets** (facial expressions), **motion capture**, **keyframe animation**, **simulation** (cloth, fluid, particles, rigid body, soft body).
- **Camera**: virtual focal length, depth of field, motion blur.
- **Styles**: photoreal CGI (fake out-of-home, "CGI billboard" trend like Maybelline mascara trains), stylized/toon shading (cel shading), claymorphism (soft, rounded, matte plastic), low-poly, voxel, wireframe, inflated / puffy 3D type, chrome and liquid metal, glass and refraction, miniature/tilt-shift dioramas.
- **Formats**: OBJ, FBX, GLB/glTF (web and AR), USD/USDZ (Apple AR, pipelines).
- **Mascots and avatars**: character sheet (front, side, back, expressions, poses), turnaround, silhouette test (recognizable in solid black), shape language (circles friendly, squares dependable, triangles dynamic or villainous), proportion (head-to-body ratio; chibi 1:2 to heroic 1:8).

## 12. Motion vocabulary (summary; full detail in file 09 and `bright-matter-brand/reference/motion-vocabulary.html`)

- **Easing**: ease-in (accelerates), ease-out (decelerates; most UI), ease-in-out, linear (mechanical), spring/bounce, overshoot.
- **Disney's 12 principles of animation** (Thomas and Johnston, *The Illusion of Life*): squash and stretch, anticipation, staging, straight ahead vs pose to pose, follow-through and overlapping action, slow in and slow out, arcs, secondary action, timing, exaggeration, solid drawing, appeal.
- **Kinetic typography, morphing, masking/reveals, match cuts, parallax, stagger, scroll-scrubbing, pinned sections, micro-interactions, loops, seamless loops, frame-by-frame, rotoscoping, stop-motion, cel animation**.
- **Motion identity**: a brand's rules for how things move (speed, easing curve, direction, rhythm).

## 13. Visual rhetoric (how images make arguments)

Advertising images persuade through structure. The academic map: **Phillips and McQuarrie (2004), "Beyond Visual Metaphor: A New Typology of Visual Rhetoric in Advertising," *Marketing Theory*** [peer-reviewed]. Two dimensions:
- **Visual structure** (how the two elements are placed): **juxtaposition** (side by side), **fusion** (combined into one hybrid object), **replacement** (one element stands in for another, which is absent).
- **Meaning operation**: **connection** (A is associated with B), **comparison for similarity** (A is like B), **comparison for opposition** (A is not like B).
- Complexity rises from juxtaposition to fusion to replacement; their research suggests more complex structures can raise elaboration and liking when viewers can still resolve them, and backfire when they can't.

Rhetorical figures in visual form:
- **Visual metaphor**: show A as B to transfer qualities (a sneaker made of feathers = light).
- **Visual pun**: an image that reads two ways.
- **Form substitution** (Jason Swet's term): the shape of one thing means another (replacement structure).
- **Metonymy**: an associated thing stands for the whole (a crown for monarchy).
- **Synecdoche**: a part for the whole (a single tire tread for the car).
- **Hyperbole**: visual exaggeration (Jason's "showing the impact of the product, the benefit").
- **Litotes / understatement**: the restrained ad that trusts the viewer.
- **Irony**: image contradicts the expected meaning.
- **Antithesis**: opposing images side by side (before/after, problem/solution).
- **Personification / anthropomorphism**: objects with human traits (M&M's).
- **Rebus**: image replaces a word.
- **Repetition and accumulation**: many of the same to signify scale (The Ordinary's "visual index").
- **Absence / deletion**: removing the expected element (logo-less ads from brands with distinctive assets).
- **Genre quotation / pastiche / parody / homage**: borrowing the codes of a known format.

## 14. Semiotics for art directors

- **Sign = signifier (the form) + signified (the meaning)** (Saussure).
- **Icon** (resembles), **index** (caused by or physically connected: smoke means fire, a footprint), **symbol** (arbitrary, learned: a dove for peace) (Peirce).
- **Denotation** (literal) vs **connotation** (associated meaning) vs **myth** (cultural ideology naturalized; Roland Barthes, *Mythologies*, and his Panzani pasta ad analysis in "Rhetoric of the Image").
- **Codes**: shared conventions a category uses (luxury codes: serif caps, black, space, slow motion; discount codes: red, yellow, starbursts, exclamation).
- **Residual, dominant, emergent codes** (Raymond Williams' cultural categories, used by commercial semiotics practitioners): what's fading, what's standard, what's rising. **Use**: map a category's dominant codes to find where to stand out, or which emergent code to adopt early.
- **Category codes vs distinctiveness**: break a category code deliberately only when you can own the replacement.
- Jason's blue ballpoint (Polaroid) is a connotation argument: blue ink signifies personal authenticity.

## 15. Gestalt principles of perception

- **Proximity**: near things group.
- **Similarity**: alike things group (color, shape, size).
- **Closure**: the mind completes incomplete shapes (IBM stripes, WWF panda).
- **Continuity / good continuation**: the eye follows smooth paths.
- **Figure/ground**: we separate object from background.
- **Common fate**: things moving together group.
- **Prägnanz / simplicity**: we perceive the simplest interpretation.
- **Common region**: items inside a boundary group.
- **Symmetry and order**.
- **Use**: logo construction, UI grouping, legible ads at speed.

## 16. Aesthetics and style labels (the 2010s to 2026 mood lexicon)

Treat these as shorthand for genre quotation. Name the lineage when using them.

- **Y2K**: chrome, bubble type, translucent plastics, iridescence, futurism from roughly 1997 to 2004. Lineage: 1960s space age, iMac G3.
- **Frutiger Aero**: glossy skeuomorphic optimism, aqua blues, bubbles, nature plus tech (mid-2000s Windows Vista era).
- **Vaporwave / synthwave / outrun**: 80s and 90s consumer nostalgia, pink and teal, Greek busts, grids, sunsets.
- **Cyberpunk**: neon, rain, dense signage, East Asian megacity codes (Blade Runner lineage).
- **Solarpunk**: optimistic eco-futurism, greenery plus clean tech, Art Nouveau curves.
- **Brutalism / neo-brutalism** (web): raw default HTML look, harsh borders, flat bright fills, offset hard shadows. Lineage: concrete Brutalist architecture (béton brut).
- **Swiss punk / acid graphics / Y3K**: chrome, distorted type, rave flyers, dark mode.
- **Memphis**: 1980s Ettore Sottsass collective, squiggles, clashing pastels, terrazzo.
- **Bauhaus revival**: primary colors, geometric shapes, sans serifs.
- **Mid-century modern**: Eames, Saul Bass, organic shapes, mustard and teal.
- **Maximalism / dopamine décor**: more is more, clashing pattern and color.
- **Minimalism / quiet luxury / old money**: restraint, neutral palettes, logos absent or tiny, serif caps.
- **Clean girl / that girl**: dewy minimal beauty and wellness codes.
- **Cottagecore, dark academia, light academia, coastal grandmother, mob wife, tomato girl summer, blokecore, gorpcore, normcore, indie sleaze, office siren**: TikTok-era fashion aesthetics with distinct palettes and props.
- **Liminal spaces / dreamcore / weirdcore / backrooms**: empty transitional spaces, uncanny nostalgia.
- **Lo-fi digicam / 2000s point-and-shoot**: direct flash, date stamps, compression.
- **Grainy gradients / mesh gradients / aura gradients**: soft color fields with noise (Skylrk via Serifa is a variant).
- **Glassmorphism, claymorphism, skeuomorphism, neumorphism, flat design, material design**: UI surface styles.
- **Editorial flash fashion**: harsh flash, deadpan expressions, odd crops.
- **Analog revival / film look**: grain, halation, imperfections as a counter to AI polish.
- **AI aesthetic** (pejorative "AI slop"): over-smooth skin, symmetrical glowing scenes, hyper-detail with no point of view. Avoid unless quoted ironically.
- **Anti-design / ugly design**: deliberate clumsiness, default fonts, clashing; signals irony and insider status.
- **Absurdist object art**: the Oatly x Nikolas Bentel "Matcha Urgency Case" mode; MSCHF; Tom Sachs' bricolage.

## 17. Critique language (how to give and frame feedback)

- **Read**: what is seen first, second, third. "The first read is the background texture, not the product."
- **Legibility vs readability**: can you identify the letters vs can you comfortably read the text.
- **Stopping power / thumb-stopping**: arrests the scroll.
- **Holding power**: keeps attention after the stop (Jason's hook test).
- **Branding / brand linkage**: will people remember whose ad it was. Weak linkage = a great ad for the category.
- **Single-minded**: communicates one thing.
- **On-brief / off-brief, on-brand / off-brand**.
- **Concept vs execution**: is the problem the idea or how it was made.
- **Ownable / distinctive**: could a competitor run it unchanged? If yes, it's not ownable.
- **Earned vs unearned**: does the payoff justify the setup.
- **Too literal / too abstract**.
- **Visual noise / clutter / "mismatched luggage"** (Jason): inconsistent visual language.
- **Tonal mismatch**: visual tone contradicts the message or brand.
- **Craft**: the quality of execution (retouching, kerning, grade, edit).
- **Restraint, tension, pacing, rhythm, breath**.
- **Lineage / reference / derivative / homage / rip-off**: where something comes from and whether it adds anything.
- Feedback form that works: **observation → effect → question or direction**. "The headline and the product compete at the same size, so nothing leads. What if the product is the only big thing?"

## 18. Presentation and deliverable vocabulary

- **Mood board** (with written rationale per Jason), **style frames** (key stills defining the look), **storyboard**, **animatic** (timed storyboard with sound), **rip-o-matic** (cut from existing footage to sell a feel), **treatment** (director's written and visual approach), **deck**, **tissue session** (early rough ideas reviewed internally), **key visual (KV)**, **hero image**, **campaign toolkit**, **brand guidelines / brand book**, **design system**, **asset matrix** (every size and format by channel), **cutdowns** (shorter edits), **lifts** (pieces of a hero film reused), **toolkit / templates**, **in situ mockups**, **pre-production book (PPM deck)**, **shot list**, **call sheet**.
